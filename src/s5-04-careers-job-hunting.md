# 5.4 Careers and job hunting with AI
<!-- original: ch. 94 -->

Job hunting is a sales job. You are the product. Your CV is the advert. Your interview is the demo. AI can help at every stage. It can also hurt you if you let it lie for you. This chapter shows the honest way to use it.

Start before you apply anywhere. Figure out what you want. AI can help you think, but it cannot choose your life. Ask it to list roles that match your skills. Ask it what those roles pay in your area. Ask what skills they need that you lack. Then decide. A clear target beats a hundred random applications.

## CVs that get read

A CV, also called a résumé, is a short record of your work and skills. Most CVs are skimmed in seconds. AI helps you make those seconds count.

Paste your job history into a chat. Ask AI to turn it into bullet points that start with strong verbs. "Built", "led", "fixed", and "grew" beat "responsible for". Ask it to cut each bullet to one line. Ask it to remove jargon a hiring manager would not know.

Then tailor the CV to each job. Read the job advert. Pick the three skills it repeats most. Make sure those skills appear in your CV, with real examples. AI can compare your CV with the advert and show the gaps. Fill the gaps with true experience. Never invent a skill you do not have. A lie on a CV is found in the interview or in the first week.

Keep one master CV with everything. Then make a short version for each application. Two pages is the max for most jobs. One page is better for early careers. Check the current advice for your industry, because norms differ.

> NOTE: Some employers use software to screen CVs before a human reads them. Use plain words from the job advert, and avoid fancy formatting that screening software cannot read. A simple layout beats a clever one.

## Cover letters, outreach, and smarter searching

Cover letters are short. Three paragraphs is enough. Paragraph one says what job you want and why this company. Paragraph two says what you offer, with one concrete example. Paragraph three says what you want next, like an interview. AI can draft this. You must rewrite it in your voice. A generic letter gets a generic rejection.

Outreach means contacting people directly, not just applying online. Ask AI to help you write a short message to someone at a company you like. Keep it under a hundred words. Say what you admire about their work. Ask one question. Do not ask for a job in the first message. People help people who are curious, not people who are pushy.

Smarter searching means letting AI widen your net. Ask it for job titles you had not thought of. Ask it which companies hire for your skills. Ask it to summarise a company's recent news before you apply. Set up alerts for new roles. Apply fast. Early applicants get read first.

> TRY IT: Take one real job advert. Paste it into a chat with your CV. Ask: what are the three most important skills here, and where does my CV prove each one? Fix the weakest proof today.

## Interview practice, the superpower

Interview practice is the best use of AI in this whole chapter. It is free. It is unlimited. It never gets tired.

Ask the AI to play the interviewer. Give it the job advert and your CV. Ask it to ask you one question at a time. Answer out loud, as you would in a real interview. Ask it to mark your answer. Was it clear? Was it specific? Did it prove the skill? Repeat the weak ones.

Use the STAR method for stories. STAR stands for Situation, Task, Action, Result. "Our team lost a client. I was asked to win them back. I called them, found the problem, and fixed it in a week. They stayed for two more years." That is a STAR answer. It has a beginning, a middle, and a result with a number. AI can help you shape your stories into STAR form. Prepare six stories. They cover most interview questions.

Practice the hard questions too. "Tell me about yourself." "Why should we hire you?" "What is your biggest weakness?" "Why did you leave your last job?" Draft honest answers. Say them out loud. Short and true beats long and polished.

Also prepare questions to ask them. "What does success look like in this role?" "What is the team working on now?" "What would you want me to achieve in ninety days?" Good questions show you care. AI can brainstorm ten. Pick three.

## Negotiating offers, and your first 90 days

An offer is not just a salary. It is salary, benefits, hours, location, and growth. AI can help you prepare. Ask it what the market rate is for the role in your area. Ask it to list the benefits worth asking about. Ask it to draft a polite counter-offer. Never accept the first offer on the spot. Say thank you. Ask for a day to think. Then negotiate once, clearly, and in writing.

Your AI skills are a career asset now. Say so in interviews. Give examples. "I use AI to draft reports, then edit them myself." "I built a small automation that saves my team two hours a week." Employers want people who use AI well, not people who fear it.

Your first 90 days matter. Ask AI to help you make a plan. Days one to thirty, listen and learn. Days thirty to sixty, take on one real project. Days sixty to ninety, deliver something visible. Write the plan down. Share it with your manager. People who plan their start get trusted faster.

## Honesty rules

The honesty rules are short and strict. Never let AI invent a job, a degree, or a skill. Never submit an AI-written cover letter without reading and rewriting it. Never use AI to cheat on a skills test. If a test says no AI, that means no AI. Never let AI write your answers during a live interview. If you are caught, you lose the job and your reputation.

AI is a coach, not a costume. It helps you present your real self clearly. It must not present a false self. The job you get by lying is a job you will lose.

Your first 90 days in a new job matter. Ask AI to help you draft a plan. Days one to thirty, listen and learn. Meet the team. Learn the systems. Days thirty to sixty, take on one real project and finish it. Days sixty to ninety, deliver something visible that helps the team. Write the plan down. Share it with your manager. People who plan their start get trusted faster.

Your AI skills are a career asset. Say so in interviews, with examples. "I use AI to draft reports, then edit them myself." "I built a small automation that saves my team two hours a week." Employers want people who use AI well. Show them you are one of those people.

## Check yourself

1. What should you do before you apply for any job?
**Answer:** Decide what you want, using AI to list matching roles, pay, and missing skills, then target clearly instead of applying at random.

2. Why tailor your CV to each job advert?
**Answer:** Because the advert repeats the skills that matter most, and your CV must prove those skills with real examples.

3. What is the STAR method?
**Answer:** A way to tell interview stories in four parts: Situation, Task, Action, and Result, with a number in the result.

4. Why is AI interview practice so valuable?
**Answer:** Because it is free, unlimited, and patient, and it can mark your answers and repeat the weak ones until they are strong.

5. What is the honesty rule for AI in job hunting?
**Answer:** AI may help you present your real self clearly, but it must never invent jobs, degrees, skills, or test answers for you.

## In short

- Decide what you want first, then target your applications clearly.
- Make CV bullets short, strong, and proven with real examples.
- Tailor each application, and keep cover letters to three honest paragraphs.
- Practise interviews with AI, answering out loud in STAR stories.
- Negotiate once, clearly, in writing, and plan your first 90 days.
- Try this week: a CV gap check, three stronger bullets, a mock interview, and two STAR stories.
