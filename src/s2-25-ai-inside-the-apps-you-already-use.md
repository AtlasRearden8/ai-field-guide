# 2.25 AI inside the apps you already use
<!-- original: ch. 53 -->

You do not always need a new tool. The apps you already live in now have serious AI built in. Notes, docs, email, spreadsheets, chat, design tools, even your phone's operating system. Many are connected to AI, so your assistant can reach in and your apps can reach out. This chapter is a guided tour of where the AI is hiding, and the best tricks in each.

Built-in AI has one big advantage. It already sees your document, sheet, or inbox. You do not copy and paste context in. Results land right where you need them. You do not copy results back out. It uses the app's own permissions and data rules.

Your main assistant with connectors has the opposite advantage. It can combine many apps at once. The sweet spot is both. Built-in AI for in-app jobs, like "rewrite this doc" or "sum up this thread." Your main assistant with connectors for cross-app jobs, like "read my email, calendar, and notes, then plan my week."

## Notes and docs

Notion has an AI agent, custom agents on schedules, AI meeting notes, database autofill, and MCP connections both ways. Obsidian has community AI plugins, local models, and MCP access to your notes vault. Google Docs has Gemini drafting, rewriting, summarising, and "help me create" from your files. Microsoft Word has Copilot drafting from files, rewriting, and Q and A on long docs. Coda, Craft, Mem, Reflect, and Capacities offer AI columns, AI-native organisation, and chat with your notes.

> TRY IT: In any doc, select a messy paragraph. Ask the built-in AI to "rewrite for clarity and half the length, keep my voice." Compare it with the original. You will use this weekly.

## Spreadsheets

Google Sheets with Gemini can generate formulas, tables, and analysis. It also has an =AI() function for per-cell prompts, one AI question per cell. Excel with Copilot can write formulas, build pivot tables, make charts, and run Python in Excel. It also has a COPILOT() function in recent versions. Airtable AI has fields that sum up, categorise, and pull out details per row.

Try this: paste 200 survey responses into a sheet. Add AI columns for sentiment, theme, and a one-line summary. Everything else is in chapter 2.28.

## Email, calendar, project, and team tools

Gmail with Gemini and Outlook with Copilot offer summaries, drafting, and smart search. AI-first email clients like Superhuman, Shortwave, and Spark add auto-sorting, AI search, and "write like me." AI calendars like Reclaim and Motion auto-schedule tasks and defend focus time. The full playbook is in chapter 2.27.

Project tools have AI too. Linear offers AI triage, duplicate detection, and agents you can assign issues to, plus an official MCP server. Asana offers AI teammates, status summaries, smart workflows, and an MCP server. ClickUp and monday.com offer workspace Q and A, auto-updates, AI fields, and official MCP servers. Slack offers channel recaps, thread summaries, enterprise search, agents, and an official MCP server. Jira and Confluence offer AI summaries, search, agents, and a remote MCP server. Miro and FigJam can cluster sticky notes, generate diagrams, and summarise boards.

## Design, meetings, and voice

Canva offers magic design, image generation, brand kits, resize for every platform, and an MCP connector. Figma offers AI design features and a Dev Mode MCP for coding agents. Adobe's Photoshop, Firefly, and Express offer generative fill, expand, and commercially safe generation. CapCut and Descript let you edit video by editing text, with auto-captions and voice cleanup.

For meetings, AI notetakers include Granola, Otter, and Fireflies, plus built-ins in Zoom, Google Meet, Teams, and Notion. Tell people when AI is taking notes. It builds trust. For dictation, Wispr Flow, Superwhisper, and built-in OS dictation turn rambling into polished text.

> NOTE: Meeting notes are powerful, and they can also be sensitive. Check your company's rules before you record a call, and tell everyone in the room.

These are the AI features you already own. Many people never turn them on. A ten-minute tour of your phone's settings can surface three or four useful tools.

## Reading and learning apps

Readwise Reader has an AI "Ghostreader" for summaries and definitions, plus highlight syncing to your notes. Kindle, Apple Books, and browser readers have built-in summaries and "explain this" features that keep improving. Perplexity Spaces and Claude or ChatGPT Projects are research workspaces with your files. Gemini Notebook turns your sources into grounded research with audio overviews.

## The hub strategy

Pick one home base for your knowledge. Notion, Obsidian, or Google Drive. Then pipe things into it automatically. Meeting notes go there. Receipts go there. Ideas go there. A single well-fed hub beats ten half-used apps. When your hub is full, AI can read it and answer questions about your life and work. That is the whole point of the next section of this book.

## Audit your apps in ten minutes

Make a small table. List five apps you use daily. For each one, write down its AI feature and one task you will try this week. Most people discover two or three features they are already paying for but never used. Do the audit, then use one newly discovered feature for a real task today. Small wins stack up into big habits.

> TRY IT: Open the app you use most for work. Search its help for "AI." Write down three features you did not know existed. Try one before lunch.

## When built-in AI is not enough

Built-in AI has limits. It usually sees one app at a time. It cannot read your email and your calendar and your notes together. It cannot run on a schedule. It cannot call tools outside its own app. That is when you add connectors or MCP. Your main assistant reads across apps. An automation runs the job while you sleep. A local model handles the private stuff. Think of it as a ladder. Built-in AI is the first rung. Connectors are the second. Automations are the third. Local AI is the fourth. Climb only as far as the job needs. Most jobs stop at rung one or two.

> NOTE: The ladder is not a race. Staying on rung one is fine if rung one does the job.

## A week of built-in AI wins

Try one win per day for a week. Monday: rewrite a messy paragraph in your notes app. Tuesday: summarise your longest email thread. Wednesday: ask your spreadsheet to write one formula for you. Thursday: let your chat app recap a busy channel. Friday: ask your phone to summarise your notifications. Saturday: generate an image for a birthday card in Canva. Sunday: ask your notes app to find everything you wrote about one topic this month. Seven small wins. Each one takes minutes. By Sunday, built-in AI will feel like a habit, not a feature list. Keep a short list of the wins that saved real time. Repeat those weekly. Drop the ones that did not help. A habit that earns its keep is a habit that lasts.

> NOTE: Features you never try are features you do not have. A ten-minute tour of one app per week is the cheapest AI upgrade there is.

## Check yourself

1. What is the main advantage of built-in AI over a standalone chatbot?
**Answer:** It already sees your document, sheet, or inbox, so you do not copy context in and out.

2. When is your main assistant with connectors better than built-in AI?
**Answer:** For cross-app tasks, like combining email, calendar, and notes into one plan.

3. What is the hub strategy?
**Answer:** Choose one home base for knowledge, pipe things into it automatically, and let AI read and write it.

4. Why should you tell people when AI is taking meeting notes?
**Answer:** It builds trust, and in many places it is legally required to tell them.

5. What does an AI function like =AI() do in a spreadsheet?
**Answer:** It runs one AI prompt per cell, so you can classify or summarise hundreds of rows at once.

## In short

- Built-in AI shines for in-app jobs. Your main assistant with connectors shines across apps.
- Notes, docs, sheets, email, chat, design, meetings, and phones all have AI now.
- Pick one knowledge hub, feed it automatically, and let AI read it.
- Audit your daily apps. You are probably sitting on unused AI features.
- Tell people when AI records a meeting, and check your company's rules first.
