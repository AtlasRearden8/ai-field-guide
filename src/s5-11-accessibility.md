# 5.11 Accessibility: tech that includes everyone
<!-- original: ch. 101 -->

Accessibility means technology that works for everyone. People see, hear, move, and think in different ways. AI can remove barriers that used to block people out. This chapter is for everyone. It shows what AI can do for different needs, and how to build things that include people.

The key idea is simple. People do not need fixing. Barriers need fixing. A video without captions blocks a deaf person. A website without descriptions blocks a blind person. A form with tiny text blocks an older person. AI can fix many of these barriers in seconds.

## The accessibility map

Start with the map. Different needs, different tools. No single tool fits everyone. The right question is always: what barrier does this person face, and what removes it?

For blind and low-vision people, AI can describe the world. It can read text aloud from a photo. It can describe a scene. It can read a menu, a letter, or a street sign. Apps like Seeing AI and Be My Eyes use AI to describe what the camera sees. A volunteer or an AI answers in seconds. This turns a phone into a pair of eyes.

For deaf and hard-of-hearing people, AI can turn sound into text. Live captions appear as people speak. They work in meetings, lectures, and videos. AI can also turn text into sign language avatars in some tools. Captions help everyone, not just deaf people. They help in noisy rooms. They help language learners. They help anyone watching without sound.

For speech and voice, AI helps in two directions. It can understand unclear speech better than older tools. It can also give a voice to people who cannot speak. Text-to-speech tools read typed words aloud in a natural voice. Some tools can build a personal voice from a short recording. That voice belongs to the person. Ask before you use anyone's voice, and never clone a voice without clear consent.

For mobility and dexterity, AI helps people control devices without fine movement. Voice control runs phones, lights, and computers. Eye tracking moves a cursor. Switch controls let one button do many jobs. AI makes these controls smarter. It predicts what you want. It forgives mistakes.

For cognitive and neurodivergent support, AI helps with focus, memory, and routine. It can break a task into small steps. It can read text aloud. It can summarise long pages. It can remind you gently. It can turn a busy page into a calm one. People with dyslexia, ADHD, or autism use these tools daily. The tools do not cure anything. They remove friction.

For older adults, AI helps with confidence. Big text. Clear voice control. Simple steps. A photo of a letter, read aloud. A reminder to take medicine. A video call that starts with one tap. The best tool for an older person is the one they will actually use. Simple wins.

> NOTE: Accessibility tools handle sensitive data. Voice, photos, and health details pass through them. Read the privacy terms. Choose tools from trusted makers. Delete data you do not need.

## Building accessible things

If you build anything, a website, a document, a video, or an app, build it accessibly. It is easier than you think. And AI can help you check your work.

Write alt text for images. Alt text is a short description of an image for people who cannot see it. AI can draft it. You check it. A good alt text says what the image shows and why it matters. "A bar chart showing sales rising from ten to fifty" is good. "Image" is not.

Caption every video. AI can generate captions in minutes. Check them. Fix names and numbers. Captions help deaf viewers and everyone watching on mute.

Use clear structure. Headings in order. Short paragraphs. Plain words. High contrast between text and background. Big enough text. These help everyone, including people with no disability at all. A clear page is a kind page.

Test with real people. AI can check many things. It cannot replace a real user. Ask someone who uses a screen reader to try your page. A screen reader is software that reads a page aloud. Their feedback is worth more than any automated score.

> TRY IT: Take one photo you posted online. Write alt text for it in one sentence. Then ask AI to write alt text for the same photo. Compare the two. Keep the one that helps a blind person understand the image best.

## Principles: respect, choice, and privacy

Three principles guide everything in this chapter. Respect. Choice. Privacy.

Respect means asking, not assuming. Do not grab someone's wheelchair. Do not speak loudly to a blind person. Do not finish a stammering person's sentences. Ask what help they want. Then listen.

Choice means offering options, not forcing one. Some people love AI help. Some prefer a human. Some want both. Let the person choose. A tool that is forced on someone is a barrier, not a help.

Privacy means the person's data stays theirs. Accessibility tools see a lot. Voices. Faces. Health notes. Never share that data without consent. Never use it to train a model without clear permission. Delete it when it is no longer needed.

## Check yourself

1. What does accessibility mean, in plain words?
**Answer:** Technology that works for everyone, by removing barriers instead of expecting people to change.

2. How does AI help blind and low-vision people?
**Answer:** It describes what the camera sees, reads text aloud from photos, letters, and signs, and turns a phone into a helper for the world.

3. What is alt text, and what makes it good?
**Answer:** A short description of an image for people who cannot see it, which says what the image shows and why it matters.

4. Why should you test with real people, not just AI checks?
**Answer:** Because AI can check many things, but only a real user, like someone with a screen reader, can tell you what actually works.

5. What are the three principles of this chapter?
**Answer:** Respect, choice, and privacy: ask what help is wanted, offer options, and keep the person's data theirs.

## In short

- Accessibility removes barriers so technology works for everyone.
- AI describes images, captions speech, gives voices, and simplifies tasks.
- Different needs need different tools, so start with the barrier, not the gadget.
- Build accessibly: alt text, captions, clear structure, and high contrast.
- Test with real people, and respect their choice and privacy.
- Try this week: live captions, alt text, one voice feature, and one page check.
