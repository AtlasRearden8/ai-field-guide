# 2.14 Same task, every assistant
<!-- original: ch. 30 -->

Which AI is best? The honest answer is: best at what, for whom?

This chapter runs ten everyday challenges across the major assistants. It explains which ones tend to shine at each task, and why. Usually the reason is a specific feature, not overall intelligence.

Then it hands you a scorecard to run your own showdown. Your taste and your tasks matter more than any ranking. Rankings change every few months anyway.

## How to read these showdowns

AI models improve constantly. Quality differences between the top assistants are often small. So instead of crowning permanent winners, each showdown tells you **what to look for** and **which features give certain assistants an edge** for that kind of task.

Run the prompts yourself. It is the only ranking that counts.

## Showdown 1: The tricky email

*"Write a short email to my landlord. The heating has been broken for 5 days. I have reported it twice with no response. I have a baby at home. Firm but polite. Mention what I will do if it is not fixed by Friday."*

**Look for:** natural tone, not robotic. The right firmness. No invented facts. A sensible next step.

**Who tends to shine:** all the major assistants do this well. Claude and ChatGPT are often praised for natural, human-sounding prose. Gemini in Gmail and Copilot in Outlook win on convenience, because they draft right where you send.

**Inkling 2 Small:** it writes a careful, well-structured email. It takes longer, because it thinks first. For a sensitive email, that care is worth it.

## Showdown 2: Research a purchase

*"Compare the 4 best air purifiers under $250 for a bedroom with a dog. Make a table: room size, filter cost per year, noise, and what reviewers complain about. Cite recent reviews."*

**Look for:** real, current products. Working source links. Honest downsides. Filter costs, which are often forgotten.

**Who tends to shine:** Perplexity, because sources are its whole identity. The deep research modes in ChatGPT, Gemini, and Claude give more thorough reports. Always click two sources before buying.

**Inkling 2 Small:** it is methodical. It lays out its reasoning and checks its own claims. Good for a careful comparison, though it has no live shopping features of its own.

## Showdown 3: What is happening right now

*"What are the three biggest news stories in my country today? For each: what happened, why it matters, and links to two reputable sources."*

**Look for:** today's date on sources. Reputable outlets. A clear line between confirmed facts and speculation.

**Who tends to shine:** Perplexity for sourced summaries. Gemini and ChatGPT search for fresh news. Grok for what people on X are saying. Remember that chatter is not confirmation.

**Inkling 2 Small:** this is not its best event. It is a thinking model, not a live news reader. It depends on the hub's search tools for fresh facts. Use Perplexity or Gemini for breaking news instead.

## Showdown 4: Understand a long document

Upload a 30-plus page PDF, like a lease, a policy, or a report. Ask: *"Summarize the key points. List anything that could cost me money or catch me out. Quote the relevant sections with page numbers."*

**Look for:** accurate quotes. Check them. Correct page numbers. Nothing important missed.

**Who tends to shine:** Claude and Gemini are known for handling long documents well. Kimi is built for very long inputs. Gemini Notebook is superb when you want answers grounded strictly in your sources.

**Inkling 2 Small:** this is home ground for a thinking model. It works through a long document carefully, step by step. It is a strong pick for a contract or a policy, if the hub lets you upload the file.

## Showdown 5: Make an image

*"A poster for a neighborhood book swap: 'BOOK SWAP, Saturday 10am, Oak Street Library.' Cozy illustration of stacked books and a cat, warm autumn colors."*

**Look for:** correctly spelled text. Every instruction followed. A style you like.

**Who tends to shine:** ChatGPT is strong at text inside images and following detailed layouts. Gemini, with Nano Banana, excels at editing your own photos consistently. Grok Imagine and Meta AI are quick and fun. Claude does not make photos.

**Inkling 2 Small:** it does not make images. It is a text thinking model. Pair it with ChatGPT or Gemini for the picture.

## Showdown 6: Make a short video

*"A 6-second video of a hot air balloon rising over misty hills at sunrise, gentle wind sounds."*

**Look for:** realistic motion. Sound that matches. No weird glitches.

**Who tends to shine:** Gemini, with Veo, makes video with sound. Grok Imagine and Meta AI create video in-app. Specialist tools like Runway and Kling go further.

**Inkling 2 Small:** it does not make video. Use it to plan the video instead: ask it to write the shot list, the script, and the sound notes. Then make the clip in Gemini or Grok.

## Showdown 7: Plan from your data

*"Look at my calendar and email for next week. What is coming up, what do I need to prepare, and is anything clashing?"*

**Look for:** accuracy about your real commitments. Nothing invented.

**Who tends to shine:** Gemini, with Personal Intelligence across Gmail and Calendar, if you live in Google. Copilot with Outlook in Microsoft 365. ChatGPT, Claude, and Mistral Vibe through connectors. The winner is whichever assistant connects to where your life actually lives.

**Inkling 2 Small:** it has no direct access to your calendar or email. It can only work with what you paste in. Paste your week's events and ask it to spot clashes. It is careful, but it is not connected.

## Showdown 8: A genuinely tricky problem

*"We are 5 adults splitting a 4-night holiday cottage costing £1,340. Two people stay only 3 nights. One person paid the £200 deposit. Work out exactly what each person owes whom. Show your working."*

**Look for:** correct maths. Clear working. Stated assumptions.

**Who tends to shine:** use the thinking or reasoning mode in any assistant. ChatGPT Thinking, Gemini's thinking modes and Deep Think, Claude extended thinking, Grok Expert, DeepSeek DeepThink. Most get this right in reasoning mode. Fast modes are where slips happen. Check the numbers yourself.

**Inkling 2 Small:** this is exactly what it is built for. It is a thinking model. It works through the maths step by step and shows its working. Compare its answer with one other assistant. If they agree, you can be more confident.

## Showdown 9: A voice conversation

In voice mode, say: *"I am going for a walk. Teach me something fascinating about the ocean. Let me interrupt with questions."*

**Look for:** a natural voice. It handles interruptions. It keeps the thread. It feels like a conversation.

**Who tends to shine:** ChatGPT voice and Gemini Live, both with camera sharing, are highly polished. Meta AI on glasses is the most hands-free. Grok offers fun personalities. Claude voice is great for thinking out loud.

**Inkling 2 Small:** it is not a voice assistant. It lives in a hub as a text model. Use it in writing, then discuss the answer out loud with ChatGPT or Gemini Live.

## Showdown 10: Build a mini-app

*"Build a simple interactive tool where I enter my household chores and family members, and it creates a fair weekly rota I can print. Cheerful design."*

**Look for:** does it actually work? Is it pleasant to use? Can you share it?

**Who tends to shine:** Claude Artifacts, which are interactive apps you can share by link. ChatGPT, with Canvas and its site-building tools. Gemini Canvas and Google AI Studio. For bigger apps, see the vibe coding chapters later in this book.

**Inkling 2 Small:** it can plan the app carefully: the rules for fairness, the layout, the edge cases. Then hand its plan to Claude or ChatGPT to build it. That relay is a good use of a thinking model.

## Run your own showdown

Here is the method. It takes one evening.

1. Pick **five tasks you actually do**. Not generic ones. Your real tasks.
2. Use the **exact same prompt** in two to four assistants, in fresh chats.
3. Score each one from 1 to 5 on these criteria: correct, clear, useful, honest about uncertainty, and easy to use.
4. Repeat every few months. Models change.

A **scorecard** is a simple table where you write the scores. Make one on paper or in a spreadsheet. Your tasks, your scores, your winner.

Do not trust a ranking you did not run yourself. A reviewer's favorite task is not your task. A model that wins at poetry may lose at spreadsheets.

## Check yourself

1. Why does this chapter not crown one permanent winner?

**Answer:** Because quality differences are often small, depend on the task and your taste, and change every few months as models update.

2. For "What is on my calendar next week and what should I prepare?", what matters most?

**Answer:** Which assistant is connected to where your calendar and email live. Gemini for Google, Copilot for Outlook, connectors for the others.

3. Which showdown is Inkling 2 Small best suited to, and why?

**Answer:** Showdown 8, the tricky maths problem, and Showdown 4, the long document. It is a thinking model built for long, careful, multi-step work.

4. Which assistant should you use for breaking news, and which one should you avoid for it?

**Answer:** Use Perplexity, Gemini, or ChatGPT search for fresh, sourced news. Avoid relying on Inkling 2 Small for breaking news. It is a thinking model, not a live news reader.

5. How do you run a fair showdown between two assistants?

**Answer:** Use the exact same prompt in both, in fresh chats, and score them on the same criteria.

## In short

- There is no single best assistant. There is a best assistant for each task and person.
- Edges usually come from features: sources for Perplexity, integration for Gemini and Copilot, long documents for Claude, Gemini, and Kimi, images and video for ChatGPT, Gemini, Grok, and Meta, and Artifacts for Claude.
- Inkling 2 Small joins the run as a thinking model. It shines at tricky problems and long documents.
- Use reasoning mode for hard problems in any assistant.
- Run your own showdown with your real tasks. Repeat it every few months.
