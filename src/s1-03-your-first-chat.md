# 1.3 Your first chat, step by step
<!-- original: ch. 03 -->

This is the chapter where you actually do it. No more reading about AI. You are going to open an assistant, type your first message, and read the answer. It takes about ten minutes. Everything is shown step by step.

If you already did the first-hour guide, you have done some of this. Good. Do it again, slowly. This time, notice each step.

## Step 1: Open your assistant

Pick the assistant you chose in Chapter 1.4, or the one from your first hour. Open its app on your phone, or its website on your computer.

You should see a mostly empty screen with a box at the bottom. That box is where you type. Above it, there may be a few suggestion chips, like "Write an email" or "Plan a trip". Ignore them for now.

> NOTE: If the screen asks you to sign in, sign in. If it asks you to pick a plan, pick the free one. You do not need to pay to learn.

## Step 2: Type your first message

Click or tap the box at the bottom. Type something real. Not a test. Not "hello". Something you actually want.

Here is a good first message. Copy it, and change the bracketed parts to fit your life:

"Hi, I'm [your name]. I have never used AI before. Please explain what you can help me with, in short, simple sentences. Then ask me what I would like to try first."

Press send. The arrow, or the return key. Wait a few seconds. Watch the words appear, one by one. That is the chatbot guessing the next word, live, as you read about in Chapter 1.2.

Read the answer. Does it make sense? Is the tone friendly? Good. You have just had your first AI conversation.

## Step 3: Ask a follow-up

Now reply to the answer, the way you would reply to a person. This is the step most beginners miss. A chat is a conversation, not a search box.

Try one of these:

- "Give me an example."
- "Make that shorter."
- "Explain that like I am ten years old."
- "What should I ask you next?"

Watch what happens. The assistant remembers what you said. It builds on it. That is the whole skill of chatting with AI: you do not have to get the first message perfect. You steer as you go.

> TRY IT: Ask your assistant to explain something you already know well, like your hobby or your job, in three short sentences. Then say: "Now explain it to a complete beginner." Notice how the answer changes. You just steered it with one sentence.

## Step 4: Try a real job

Now use it for something real. Pick one small job from your life today. Here are some good first jobs:

- "Write a polite text to my neighbour, thanking them for the parcel they took in."
- "Plan a simple dinner for tonight using chicken, rice, and broccoli."
- "Summarise this in plain English:" then paste a short email or message.
- "Give me three ideas for a weekend walk near [your town]."

Read the answer carefully. Is it useful? Is anything wrong? Edit it in your head, or ask the assistant to fix it: "Make it warmer." "Add a shopping list." "Shorter, please."

When you are happy, use the result. Send the text. Cook the dinner. That is the point. A finished job is the win.

## Step 5: Save what worked

When you get a good answer, do not let it vanish. Save it.

Most assistants let you copy an answer, star a chat, or rename it. Rename the chat with a useful name, like "Dinner plan" or "Neighbour text". That way you can find it again.

Better still, start a note on your phone called "My prompts". Paste in the messages that worked well. Over time, this becomes your personal cookbook. Chapter 1.8 gives you many more prompts to add to it.

## Your second chat: going deeper

Your first chat was a hello. Your second chat can be a real conversation. Here is how to go deeper without getting lost.

Start with a topic you care about. Not a test. A hobby, a problem, a plan. Ask a question. Then keep asking. "Why?" "Give me an example." "What would you do differently?" A chatbot is patient. It will not get bored. Use that.

Try a longer back-and-forth. Ten turns, not two. Notice how the answers get better as the AI learns what you care about. That is the conversation doing its work.

Also learn the small tools in the sidebar. **Rename** a chat so you can find it again: "Dinner plan" is better than "New chat". **Star** the chats you want to keep. **Delete** the ones that went nowhere. A tidy sidebar is a tidy mind.

And try editing your own message. Most apps let you tap your message and change it, then get a fresh answer. This is faster than typing a correction. Change one word, and the whole answer changes. That is a powerful lesson in how much your words matter.

> TRY IT: Open a chat from earlier today. Rename it with a useful name. Star it if it was helpful. Delete one chat you do not need. You have just learned chat hygiene, and your future self will thank you.

## What you will see on screen

Every assistant looks a little different. But they all share the same parts. Here is what to look for.

| What you see | What it does |
|---|---|
| The message box | Where you type or speak |
| The send button | Sends your message |
| The microphone | Lets you talk instead of typing |
| The plus or paperclip | Adds a photo or a file |
| The sidebar | Lists your past chats |
| The new chat button | Starts a fresh, empty conversation |

If you cannot find one of these, look for a small icon, or tap the menu. They are all there. They just hide in different corners.

## When it goes wrong

Your first chat might not be perfect. That is normal. Here is what to do about the common problems.

**The answer is too long.** Say: "Make it half as long." Or: "Give me the main points in five bullets."

**The answer is wrong.** Say: "That is not right. The correct answer is..." Then ask it to try again. Or check the fact yourself and move on.

**The answer is vague.** Add detail. Say who it is for, what you want back, and how long it should be. Chapter 1.6 teaches this properly.

**It keeps getting worse.** Start a new chat. Paste in a short summary of what you need. A clean desk fixes most muddles.

> NOTE: You cannot break anything by typing. There is no wrong question. The worst thing that can happen is a bad answer, and you can always ask again or start over.

## Check yourself

1. What are the five steps of your first chat?
**Answer:** Open the assistant, type a real first message, ask a follow-up, try a real job, and save what worked.

2. Why is a follow-up message so important?
**Answer:** Because a chat is a conversation. The assistant remembers what you said, so you can steer the answer instead of getting the first message perfect.

3. What should you do when an answer is too long?
**Answer:** Ask for it shorter, or ask for the main points in a few bullets.

4. What is the fastest fix when a chat keeps getting worse?
**Answer:** Start a new chat and paste in a short summary of what you need.

5. Why should you save messages that worked well?
**Answer:** So you can reuse them later. They become your personal prompt cookbook.

## In short

- Open the app, type a real message, and press send.
- Reply to the answer like you would to a person.
- Use it for a real job, not a test.
- Save what worked, in a note called "My prompts".
- Too long, wrong, or vague? Ask for a fix, or start a new chat.
- You cannot break anything by typing.
