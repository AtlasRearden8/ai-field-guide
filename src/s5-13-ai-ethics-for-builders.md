# 5.13 AI ethics for builders
<!-- original: ch. 107 -->

Ethics sounds heavy. It sounds like philosophy class. For builders, it is simpler. Ethics means building things you are proud of. Things that are honest. Things that respect people. Things you would be happy to explain to your family. This chapter is a practical guide, not a lecture.

A builder is anyone who makes something with AI. An app. A workflow. A post. A business. If you build it, you own its effects. "The AI did it" is not an excuse. You chose the tool. You chose the settings. You pressed publish. That is the rule that runs this chapter.

## Honesty and disclosure

Honesty comes first. Tell people when they are talking to AI. A chatbot that pretends to be human is a lie. A short line fixes it. "Hi, I am an AI assistant." That is all it takes.

Disclose AI help where it matters. If AI wrote most of an article, say so. If AI made an image, label it. If AI graded a test, tell the people graded. Readers, customers, and students deserve to know what they are looking at.

Do not fake people. Do not make a fake review. Do not make a fake testimonial. Do not make a fake expert quote. Do not make a video of a real person saying things they never said. A deepfake is a fake video or voice that looks real. Deepfakes can ruin lives. Never make one of a real person without their clear consent. Never share one.

Be honest about limits. If your tool is wrong ten percent of the time, say so. If it only works in English, say so. If it needs a human to check its work, say so. Honest limits build trust. Hidden limits break it.

> NOTE: Laws about AI are forming in many countries. Rules on disclosure, data, and deepfakes are changing fast. Check the current rules where you live and where your users live, before you ship.

## Consent: faces, voices, data, and work

Consent means clear permission. Ask before you use someone's face, voice, data, or work. "Clear" means they understood what you asked. Silence is not consent. A buried checkbox is not consent.

Faces and voices need special care. Never clone a voice without the person's clear consent. Never use someone's photo to train a model without permission. Never build a tool that identifies people in photos without a good reason and clear consent. People own their faces and voices. Treat them that way.

Data needs care too. If people give you their data, use it only for what you said you would. Do not sell it. Do not share it. Do not use it to train a model unless you said so plainly and they agreed. Delete data you no longer need. A small store of data is a smaller risk.

Respecting creators is part of consent. Writers, artists, and musicians made the work AI learned from. Do not pass off their style as your own. Do not copy their work and sell it. If you use AI to make art, say so. If you train a model on someone's work, get permission or do not do it. Pay creators when you use their work. Credit them when you build on it.

## Fairness and bias

AI learns from data. Data carries human bias. Bias means unfair treatment of a group of people. If the data is biased, the AI will be biased too. Your job is to catch it before your users do.

Test with different people in mind. Does your hiring tool treat all names fairly? Does your image tool show only one kind of person as a doctor? Does your chatbot answer differently for different accents? Test it. Ask people from different groups to try it. Listen to what they find.

Fix what you find. Change the data. Change the prompt. Add a human check. If you cannot fix a bias, do not ship the feature. A biased tool that ships is worse than a fair tool that waits.

Fairness also means access. Can a person with a screen reader use your tool? Can a person on a slow connection use it? Can a person who speaks another language use it? Building for everyone is not extra work. It is the work.

## Environment, rules, and the checklist

AI uses energy. Big models use a lot of it. You cannot fix the whole industry. You can make small choices. Use a smaller model when it is enough. Cache answers instead of asking twice. Turn off tools you do not use. Small choices add up across many users.

The rules taking shape matter too. Many places now require disclosure of AI content. Some require risk checks for high-stakes tools. Some limit how personal data is used. You do not need to be a lawyer. You need to check the current rules before you ship, and to keep records of what you decided and why.

Here is the builder's ethics checklist. Use it before every launch.

- [ ] Did I tell users when they are talking to AI?
- [ ] Did I get clear consent for faces, voices, and data?
- [ ] Did I test for bias with different groups of people?
- [ ] Did I credit creators and respect their work?
- [ ] Did I say what the tool cannot do?
- [ ] Did I keep private data private and delete what I do not need?
- [ ] Would I be proud to explain this to my family?

If any answer is no, fix it before you ship. A launch can wait. Trust cannot be rebuilt quickly.

## Check yourself

1. Why is "the AI did it" not an excuse?
**Answer:** Because you chose the tool, the settings, and to press publish, so you own the effects of what you built.

2. What is disclosure, and when does it matter?
**Answer:** Telling people when they are talking to AI or looking at AI-made content, so they know what they are dealing with.

3. What is consent, in plain words?
**Answer:** Clear permission where the person understood what they agreed to, which silence or a buried checkbox does not give.

4. How do you test for bias in something you built?
**Answer:** Try it with different names, groups, and accents in mind, ask people from different groups to test it, and listen to what they find.

5. What is the builder's ethics checklist for?
**Answer:** A short list of questions to answer before every launch, covering disclosure, consent, bias, credit, limits, and privacy.

## In short

- Build things you are proud to explain to your family.
- Tell people when they are talking to AI, and label AI-made content.
- Get clear consent for faces, voices, data, and creative work.
- Test for bias with different people, and fix what you find before you ship.
- Keep private data private, and use small models when they are enough.
- Try this week: label one thing, check one tool, test for bias, and run the checklist.
