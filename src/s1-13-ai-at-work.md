# 1.13 AI at work, for beginners
<!-- original: ch. 13 -->

You do not need to be a tech worker to use AI at work. You need an inbox, a calendar, and a few dull jobs. AI helps with the dull jobs. This chapter gives you small work wins you can use on Monday morning.

## Emails: the fastest win

Email is where most people waste the most time. AI can cut that time in half.

**Drafting.** Give it the facts and the tone. "Write a short, polite email to the team saying the Friday meeting is moved to Monday at 10am. Keep it friendly." Read it. Edit it until it sounds like you. Send it.

**Replying.** Paste in a long email. Ask: "Summarise this in three bullets. What do they want from me?" Then: "Draft a reply saying yes, but I need it by Thursday."

**Tone.** "Rewrite this to sound warmer but still professional." "Make this firmer. I have asked twice." Tone is hard to get right when you are tired. AI is good at it.

> NOTE: Never paste confidential work information into a personal AI account. If your company has its own AI tool, use that for work documents. If you are unsure, ask your manager or IT team what is allowed.

## Notes and summaries

Meetings produce notes. Notes produce work. AI shortens the chain.

Paste your rough meeting notes into the assistant. Ask: "Turn this into a clear summary: decisions made, actions, and who does what by when. Use a table."

It works for articles too. "Summarise this article in five bullets, in plain English." And for long documents: "What are the three main points of this report?"

A good summary saves everyone time. A bad summary misleads everyone. Always read the summary before you share it. Check names, dates, and numbers.

## First drafts of anything

AI is a first-draft machine. Reports, proposals, job adverts, social media posts, training notes, newsletters. Give it the facts. Get a draft. Then make it yours.

The draft is not the finished work. You are. Your knowledge, your judgement, and your voice turn a generic draft into something good. AI does the blank-page part. You do the thinking part.

> TRY IT: Take one dull work job you have this week: a report, a post, or a set of notes. Ask the AI for a first draft. Time how long it takes you to turn that draft into something you would send. Compare it with doing it from scratch. Most people save half their time.

## Your calendar and your day

AI can help you plan a day, not just write about one.

"Here is my day: a meeting at 9, lunch at 12, a deadline at 4. I need 90 minutes of quiet work. Plan my day in a table, with breaks."

It can also help you prepare. "I have a meeting with a new client tomorrow. Give me five good questions to ask them." Or: "Help me prepare a 2-minute update for the team meeting."

## Writing job adverts and team updates

Two more work jobs where AI earns its keep: job adverts and team updates.

A job advert needs to be clear, welcoming, and honest. Give the AI the facts: the role, the hours, the pay range, the skills that matter, and one thing that makes the team good to work with. Ask for a short advert, in plain words, with no jargon. Then read it as if you were applying. Would you apply? If not, say what is missing.

A team update is the same shape, smaller. "Write a 5-line update for the team: what we finished this week, what is next, and one thank-you." Short updates, sent regularly, keep a team together better than long reports sent rarely.

Both jobs share a rule: the AI writes the shape, you add the truth. Only you know what the team is really like, or what the job really involves. Put that in yourself, in your own words, and the result will sound human. Because it is.

## What not to do at work

AI at work has rules. Learn them before you need them.

- **Do not share secrets.** Customer data, salaries, passwords, unreleased plans. If it is confidential, it does not go in a chatbot.
- **Do not present AI work as only yours.** If AI wrote a big part of a document, say so, if your workplace expects that. Honesty is safer than being caught.
- **Do not trust it with facts.** Check names, figures, and quotes before a document goes out. Your name is on it, not the AI's.
- **Do not let it decide for you.** Hiring, firing, pay, and discipline are human decisions. AI can help you prepare. It cannot decide.

## Saying it right: difficult work messages

Some work messages are hard to write. Asking for help. Giving feedback. Saying no to extra work. Pushing back on a deadline. AI is good at these, because it is calm and it has no ego.

Try this shape: "Help me write a [message] about [situation]. I want to sound [tone]. The key facts are [facts]."

Here is an example. "Help me write a message to my manager. I need to say I cannot take on the new project this week because my current deadline is Friday. I want to sound helpful, not refusing. Suggest an alternative: I can start on Monday."

Read the draft. Then steer it. "Make it shorter." "Make it more direct." "Add a line offering to help find someone else."

Two rules for difficult messages. First, always read the draft as if you were the person receiving it. Would you feel respected? Second, never send a message about a person to that person without reading it twice. AI cannot know your office politics. You can.

A useful trick: ask the AI to predict the reply. "How might my manager react to this message, and what should I say next?" Preparing for the reply makes the first message braver and kinder.

## The Monday morning routine

Here is a simple routine. It takes fifteen minutes. It changes your week.

1. Paste in your calendar or your task list. Ask: "What are my three most important jobs today?"
2. Draft the hardest email first, with AI's help. Send it.
3. Summarise anything long that arrived overnight.
4. Ask: "What should I prepare for my meetings today?"

Do that every Monday. By Friday, you will wonder how you worked without it.

**Answer:** Email: drafting, replying, summarising long threads, and fixing tone.

2. How should you turn meeting notes into something useful?
**Answer:** Paste them in and ask for decisions, actions, and owners, in a table.

3. Who does the thinking part when AI writes a first draft?
**Answer:** You do. AI handles the blank page. Your knowledge and judgement make it good.

4. What work information should never go into a personal AI account?
**Answer:** Confidential information: customer data, salaries, passwords, and unreleased plans.

5. What are the four steps of the Monday morning routine?
**Answer:** Find your three most important jobs, draft and send the hardest email, summarise what arrived overnight, and prepare for your meetings.

## In short

- Email is the fastest win: drafts, replies, summaries, tone.
- AI turns rough notes into clear summaries with owners and dates.
- Use AI for first drafts. You add the thinking and the voice.
- AI can plan your day and help you prepare for meetings.
- Never share confidential work information in a personal AI account.
- Check facts before anything goes out under your name.
