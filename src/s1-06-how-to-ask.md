# 1.6 How to ask so AI understands you
<!-- original: ch. 06 -->

A good question gets a good answer. A vague question gets a vague answer. That is the whole secret of prompting. This chapter teaches you the skill that matters most in this book: how to ask.

A **prompt** is what you type or say to the AI. It is your question, your request, or your instruction. Learning to write good prompts is like learning to give good directions. Clear directions get you where you want to go.

## Why vague fails

Look at these two prompts. Both ask for the same thing. Only one works well.

Vague: "Things to do in Lisbon."

Clear: "Plan a relaxed 2-day trip to Lisbon for two adults in May. We like food markets, old trams, and sea views. We do not like crowds or museums. Keep walking under 30 minutes at a time. Give me a day-by-day plan with times."

The vague prompt gives you a generic list. The clear prompt gives you a plan made for you. The difference is not the AI. It is the information you gave it.

> NOTE: The AI cannot read your mind. It can only read your words. Everything it needs must be in the prompt.

## The five parts of a good prompt

A strong prompt has five parts. You do not always need all five. But when an answer disappoints you, one of these is usually missing.

1. **Task.** What do you want? "Write an email." "Plan a trip." "Explain this."
2. **Context.** The background. Who is it for? What is the situation? "For my boss, who is busy." "For two adults and a dog."
3. **Details.** The facts that matter. Dates, limits, likes, dislikes. "Under 30 minutes." "Budget-friendly." "No mushrooms."
4. **Format.** What should the answer look like? "Five bullets." "A table." "Under 100 words."
5. **Tone.** How should it sound? "Friendly." "Formal." "Funny." "Plain and simple."

Put them together and you get prompts like this:

"Write a short, friendly email [task] to my neighbour [context], thanking them for taking in a parcel [details]. Keep it under 80 words [format], warm but not over the top [tone]."

That is a great prompt. It is also easy to write, once you know the five parts.

## Before and after

Watch the same job, asked two ways.

**Before:** "How do I save money?"

The answer will be a generic list: spend less, save more, make a budget. True, and useless.

**After:** "I earn £2,000 a month after tax. My rent is £800, bills are £300, and food is about £250. I want to save £200 a month for a holiday in six months. Give me a simple weekly plan in a table, with three small changes I can make this week."

Now the answer is yours. It uses your numbers. It gives you a plan, not a lecture.

> TRY IT: Take a question you asked AI recently, or one you are about to ask. Rewrite it with all five parts: task, context, details, format, tone. Send the new version. Compare the answers. Keep the better prompt in your notes.

## Give examples when you can

If you want the AI to write in a certain style, show it. Paste in an example and say: "Write like this."

For example: "Here is an email I wrote last week: [paste it]. Write a new email in the same style, about [your topic]."

Examples are the fastest way to get the tone right. One example beats a paragraph of description.

## Say what you do not want

Telling the AI what to avoid is just as useful as telling it what to do. "No jargon." "No exclamation marks." "Do not mention prices." "Keep it under 100 words."

Negative instructions work well. Use them.

## Ask for the answer in the shape you need

Format is the part beginners skip, and it is the easiest win. The same facts, in a different shape, are a different answer.

A table is best for comparisons: prices, options, pros and cons. Bullets are best for lists and steps. A short paragraph is best for a message. A numbered list is best for instructions in order.

Tell the AI the shape you want, and how long it should be. "Give me 5 bullets, each under 15 words." "Make a table with 3 columns: option, cost, and best for." "Write it as a story, under 200 words."

If the shape is wrong, change it. "Now turn that into a table." "Now make it a checklist I can tick off." Reshaping is free, and it takes seconds. A good answer in the wrong shape is still a bad answer. Ask for the shape that fits the job.

> NOTE: Length is part of format. "Under 100 words" is a real instruction, and the AI will try to follow it. Use it whenever a short answer matters.

## One job per prompt

Do not cram three jobs into one message. "Plan my trip, write my out-of-office email, and explain this bill" will give you three rushed answers.

Ask one thing. Get a good answer. Then ask the next thing. Chats are cheap. Split the work.

## Practice: fix these three weak prompts

The best way to learn prompting is to fix weak prompts. Here are three. Read the weak version. Then read the fix. Notice what was added.

**Weak:** "Write about dogs."

**Fixed:** "Write a short, warm paragraph for a birthday card for my friend who just adopted a rescue dog called Biscuit. Mention that Biscuit is shy but loves cheese. Keep it under 60 words, funny but kind."

What changed? A task, a person, details, a format, and a tone. The weak prompt could mean anything. The fixed prompt can only mean one thing.

**Weak:** "Help me with my CV."

**Fixed:** "I am applying for a shop assistant job. I have worked in a cafe for two years and I volunteer at a food bank on Saturdays. Rewrite my CV summary in three short bullets. Plain words. Focus on customer service and reliability. Here is my current summary: [paste it]."

What changed? The job, the experience, the format, and the example. The AI now has facts to work with, not guesses.

**Weak:** "Is this a good idea?"

**Fixed:** "I want to start a small weekend market stall selling homemade jam. I can spend £200 to start, and I have 6 hours a week. Be honest: what are the three biggest risks, and what would you do first? Give me a simple yes-or-no style verdict at the end, with reasons."

What changed? The idea, the limits, and the exact answer wanted. "Be honest" and "give me a verdict" tell the AI what kind of answer you need.

Now try it yourself. Take one prompt you wrote this week. Rewrite it with the five parts. Send both versions to your assistant. Keep the one that works better.

## The prompt formula to remember

If you remember one thing from this chapter, remember this shape:

**"Do [task], for [who], with [details]. Give it back as [format], in a [tone] tone."**

Say it in your own words. You do not need the exact shape. You need the habit: task, context, details, format, tone. Check for the missing part whenever an answer disappoints you.

## Check yourself

1. What is a prompt?
**Answer:** What you type or say to the AI: your question, request, or instruction.

2. What are the five parts of a good prompt?
**Answer:** Task, context, details, format, and tone.

3. Why did "Things to do in Lisbon" give a weak answer?
**Answer:** It had no context, no details, no format, and no tone. The AI had to guess everything.

4. Why do examples help the AI match your style?
**Answer:** Showing it an example is faster and clearer than describing a style in words.

5. Why should you ask one job per prompt?
**Answer:** Because cramming three jobs into one message gives three rushed answers. One job at a time gets a better result.

## In short

- Vague in, vague out. Clear in, clear out.
- A good prompt has task, context, details, format, and tone.
- The AI cannot read your mind. Put what it needs in your words.
- Show an example when you want a certain style.
- Say what you do not want, as well as what you do want.
- One job per prompt. Split the work across messages.
