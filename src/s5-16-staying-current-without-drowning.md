# 5.16 Staying current without drowning
<!-- original: ch. 110 -->

AI news never stops. Every day brings a new model, a new tool, a new panic. You cannot read it all. You do not need to. This chapter gives you a small routine that keeps you up to date in minutes a day. Calm, not drowning.

The fear behind this chapter is real. People worry they will fall behind. They worry a new tool will make their skills useless. Here is the truth. The basics change slowly. Good questions, clear thinking, and checking facts mattered last year. They will matter next year. New tools change the surface. They do not change the skill underneath.

## The sustainable AI diet

A diet is a plan for what you consume. An AI diet is a plan for what news you read. The goal is enough, not everything.

Pick three sources and no more. One newsletter. One trusted blog or site. One person or community you respect. Three sources is enough. Thirty sources is drowning. Read them at a set time, not all day. Fifteen minutes in the morning is plenty.

Ignore the rest. You do not need to watch every launch video. You do not need to read every hot take. Most AI news is noise. Noise is loud and useless. Signal is quiet and useful. Your three sources are your signal. Everything else is noise until proven otherwise.

Set a rule for new tools. Try one new tool a month, at most. One tool, one real job, one week of use. If it saves time, keep it. If not, drop it. A tool you tried and dropped is not a failure. It is research. A tool you installed and forgot is clutter.

> NOTE: Hype is designed to make you feel behind. A headline that says "everything has changed" usually means one small thing changed. Wait a day before you believe it. Most hype fades in a week.

## Newsletters, blogs, courses, and your own news feed

Newsletters are the best first source. They arrive on a schedule. They summarise the week. They save you from scrolling. Pick one general AI newsletter and one for your own field. Read the summaries. Click only what matters to you. Unsubscribe from any newsletter you skip three weeks in a row. An unread newsletter is guilt, not learning.

Blogs and sites are your second source. Pick one that explains things clearly. Read it when you have a question, not out of duty. A site you search is more useful than a site you skim.

Courses and learning paths are for deep dives. Take a course when you need a skill, not when you feel behind. A course with a project beats a course with only videos. Build something during the course. The project is the learning.

Automate your own AI news feed. This is a fun project. Use a tool like n8n or a simple RSS reader. RSS is a way to follow sites without visiting them. Collect your three sources in one place. Ask AI to summarise the week's news into five bullets every Friday. Read the five bullets. That is your week, in one minute. This is Section 4 territory, and it is worth building once you are comfortable there.

## Filtering the hype

Hype has tells. Learn them. "Revolutionary" usually means small. "Game-changing" usually means a demo. "Everyone is switching" usually means a few people tried it. A launch video is not proof. A benchmark is not proof. Real use by real people is proof.

Ask four questions about any big claim. Who is saying this, and what do they sell? What did they actually test? What did they leave out? What do independent users say a week later? If the claim fails these questions, wait. Waiting costs nothing. Believing hype costs time and money.

Keep a learning log. A learning log is a short note of what you tried and what you learned. One line per week is enough. "Tried tool X for email. Saved twenty minutes. Kept it." "Read about model Y. Not useful for me yet." The log stops you from re-learning the same lesson. It also shows your progress. In six months, the log will surprise you. You learned more than you thought.

The skills that age well are the ones this book teaches. Asking good questions. Checking facts. Explaining clearly. Building small useful things. Helping others. Those skills survive every new model. Learn them deeply. Chase tools lightly.

Set boundaries around AI news. Do not read it in bed. Do not read it all day. Pick one time, morning or lunch, and read then. Turn off notifications from AI news apps. Notifications are designed to pull you back. You do not need to be pulled back. Fifteen minutes a day, at a set time, is a routine. All day, in every gap, is an addiction. The difference is the clock, not the content.

Social media is the worst source of AI news. It rewards the loudest claim, not the truest one. A viral post is not evidence. If a claim matters to you, check it in your three trusted sources. If they do not mention it, it can wait. Most viral AI claims are forgotten in a week. Your time is worth more than a stranger's hot take.

## Check yourself

1. Why can you not read all the AI news?
**Answer:** Because it never stops, and you do not need to, since the basics change slowly and three good sources are enough.

2. What is the sustainable AI diet?
**Answer:** A plan to read three trusted sources for fifteen minutes a day, and ignore the rest until proven useful.

3. What is the rule for trying new tools?
**Answer:** Try one new tool a month on one real job for one week, keep it only if it saves time, and drop it otherwise.

4. How do you filter hype?
**Answer:** Ask who is saying it and what they sell, what they actually tested, what they left out, and what independent users say a week later.

5. What is a learning log, and why keep one?
**Answer:** A short weekly note of what you tried and learned, which stops you re-learning lessons and shows your real progress.

## In short

- AI news never stops, and you do not need to read it all.
- Three trusted sources and fifteen minutes a day are enough.
- Try one new tool a month on a real job, and keep it only if it saves time.
- Filter hype by asking who benefits, what was tested, and what users say later.
- Keep a one-line learning log, and invest in skills that age well.
- Try this week: pick three sources, unsubscribe from five, try one tool, and start a log.
