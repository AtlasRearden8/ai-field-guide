# 1.4 Picking your first assistant
<!-- original: ch. 04 -->

There are many AI assistants. Their names fill the news: ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Copilot, Grok, Perplexity, Meta AI, and more. They all do the same basic job. They are not all the same.

This chapter helps you pick one to start with. It also tells you the most freeing fact in this book: you can switch later. Picking one today costs nothing.

## What is an assistant, exactly?

An assistant is a chatbot with a name, an app, and a company behind it. The chatbot is the part you talk to. The company builds the model, runs the app, and sets the price.

Each assistant has a **model** inside it. The model is the brain. ChatGPT runs on GPT models, made by OpenAI. Gemini runs on Gemini models, made by Google. Claude runs on Claude models, made by Anthropic. The names matter less than the experience. What matters is: does it understand you, does it write the way you like, and does it work on your devices?

## The big names, in plain words

Here is a quick tour. You do not need to remember all of this. Just find the one that fits your life.

**ChatGPT, by OpenAI.** The most famous one. Good all-rounder. Strong at writing, coding, and images. It has voice chat and image tools built in. A good first pick if you cannot decide.

**Gemini, by Google.** Best if you live in Google's world: Android phone, Gmail, Google Docs, YouTube. It can read your Google files and help with them. It is built into many Android phones.

**Claude, by Anthropic.** Warm, clear, thoughtful writing. Excellent with long documents. Many people say its answers feel the most human. A great pick for writing and careful work.

**Copilot, by Microsoft.** Best if you live in Microsoft's world: Windows, Outlook, Word, Excel. It is built into Windows and Microsoft 365. It can help inside those apps.

**Perplexity, by Perplexity.** Best for research and current facts. It searches the web and shows its sources with links. Pick it when you want answers you can check.

**Grok, by xAI.** Fast, bold, and built into X (formerly Twitter). It knows what is happening on X right now. Fun, but not the calmest first pick.

**Meta AI, by Meta.** Lives inside WhatsApp, Instagram, and Facebook. Handy because it is already where you chat. Good for quick questions and images.

**DeepSeek, Mistral, Qwen, and Kimi.** Strong assistants from other companies and countries. DeepSeek is known for smart reasoning at a low cost. Mistral is European and privacy-minded. Qwen and Kimi come from China and are strong at many tasks. Worth a look later, once you know the basics.

> NOTE: You do not need to try them all. Pick one. Learn it well. The skills transfer. A good prompt works in every assistant.

## Free or paid? Start free

Every major assistant has a free plan. The free plan is enough to learn. It is enough for most daily jobs, too.

Paid plans give you more: faster answers, the newest models, more messages per day, bigger files, and extra tools. They cost money every month, so check the current price before you pay. Prices change often, and they differ by country.

Do not pay on day one. Use the free plan for two weeks. Then ask yourself: did I hit a limit? Did it say "come back later"? Did I want a feature I could not use? If the answer is no, keep the free plan. If yes, one paid plan is enough. You do not need to pay for three.

> TRY IT: Run the same small task through two assistants. Ask both: "Write a friendly 3-sentence thank-you note to a teacher." Compare them. Which tone do you like more? Which one understood you better? That feeling is your real test, and it takes two minutes.

## What to try before you settle

Give your first pick a fair trial. One week. Use it every day. Then judge it on four things.

1. **Did it understand what I wanted?** Or did you have to rephrase everything?
2. **Do I like the tone?** Some assistants are chatty. Some are crisp. Pick the voice you enjoy reading.
3. **Does it work where I work?** On your phone, your computer, and inside the apps you already use.
4. **Did it help with real jobs?** Not tests. Real emails, real plans, real questions.

If it fails on two or more, try another one. Switching takes five minutes. Your prompts and your skills come with you.

## Switching assistants later

Switching is easy. It takes five minutes. Here is how to do it well.

First, install the new assistant and make an account, just as you did the first time. Fill in "About me" again. Turn on two-step verification again. Your settings do not move between companies. Each assistant starts fresh.

Second, bring your best prompts with you. Open your "My prompts" note. Copy your three favourite prompts into the new assistant and try them. If they work, you are home. If they do not, adjust them. Prompts are yours. They travel with you.

Third, do not delete the old assistant yet. Keep both for a week. Use the new one for daily jobs. Keep the old one for anything important. After a week, judge the new one with the same four tests: understanding, tone, devices, and real results. Then pick one, or keep both.

Your chats do not move between assistants. That is the one thing you lose. Save anything important before you switch: copy good answers into your notes. Your prompts, your skills, and your judgement all come with you. Those are the parts that matter.

## You can use more than one

Here is a secret that experienced users know: no single assistant wins at everything. Many people use two or three. One for writing. One for research. One that lives in their phone.

That is fine. It is also for later. Right now, one is enough. Learn one deeply. Add a second when you have a clear reason, like "I need sources for this research" or "I want better help inside Gmail".

## Check yourself

1. What is the difference between an assistant and a model?
**Answer:** The model is the brain inside. The assistant is the named app, built by a company, that you talk to.

2. Which assistant suits someone who uses Gmail and Google Docs all day?
**Answer:** Gemini, because it connects to Google apps.

3. Which assistant is best for answers with sources you can check?
**Answer:** Perplexity, because it searches the web and shows links.

4. Should you pay for a plan on day one?
**Answer:** No. Start free for two weeks. Pay only if you hit a limit or need a feature you cannot use.

5. What four things should you judge your assistant on after a week?
**Answer:** Whether it understood you, whether you like its tone, whether it works on your devices, and whether it helped with real jobs.

## In short

- Many assistants exist. They all chat. They differ in strengths and homes.
- Match the assistant to your life: Google, Microsoft, writing, or research.
- Start free. Pay only if you hit a limit.
- Judge it after a week of real use, on understanding, tone, devices, and results.
- You can switch any time, and you can use more than one later.
- The skills transfer. A good prompt works everywhere.
