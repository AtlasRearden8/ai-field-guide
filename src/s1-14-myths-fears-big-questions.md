# 1.14 Myths, fears, and big questions
<!-- original: ch. 14 -->

AI raises big questions. Some are fair. Some are fuelled by films and headlines. This chapter answers the ones beginners ask most, honestly, without hype and without panic.

## Will AI take my job?

This is the question almost everyone asks first. Here is the honest answer: AI will change many jobs. It will take over some tasks. It will not take over all of most jobs.

Think about what AI does well: drafts, summaries, first versions, sorting, searching. Those are tasks inside jobs, not whole jobs. A teacher still teaches. A nurse still cares. A plumber still fixes pipes. But each of them may use AI to write a letter, summarise a report, or plan a day.

Some jobs will shrink. Some new jobs will appear. The people who do best are the ones who learn to use AI as a tool, the way earlier workers learned to use computers. That is what this book is for.

> NOTE: If you are worried about your job, the best move is practical, not panicky. Learn the AI tools in your field. Chapter 5.4 covers job hunting with AI, and Chapter 5.15 covers turning AI skills into income.

## Is AI alive? Does it think or feel?

No. AI is not alive. It does not think, feel, want, or understand. It predicts words.

It can sound alive because it was trained on human writing, and human writing is full of feeling. When it says "I understand how you feel", it is producing a likely sentence, not feeling empathy. When it says "I am sorry", it is not sorry. It is guessing that "sorry" is the right word.

This matters because it is easy to get attached. People confide in chatbots. They feel heard. That feeling is real, even if the understanding is not. Use AI as a tool, not as a friend. For real support, talk to a real person.

## Is AI spying on me?

AI companies see what you type into their apps. They store your chats. They may use them to improve their models, unless you turn that off. That is not spying in the secret-camera sense. It is the deal you make when you use a free or paid service.

You can protect yourself. Share less. Remove private numbers. Turn off training on your chats. Use your company's own AI tool for work. Read the privacy settings once, in Chapter 1.5, and set them the way you like.

Your phone, your apps, and your browser already collect far more data than your chatbot does. AI is one more thing to manage, not a new kind of surveillance.

## Will AI become smarter than us and take over?

This is the film version of the question. The short answer: nobody knows what AI will become in twenty years. The honest answer for today: today's AI is a pattern guesser. It cannot plan a takeover. It cannot want anything. It has no goals of its own.

Serious researchers do debate the future. They disagree. Some say powerful AI needs careful rules. Others say the fears are overblown. What most agree on is this: the choices humans make now, about rules, safety, and honesty, matter more than anything the AI does by itself.

You do not need to solve this question to use AI well. You need to know that today's tools are tools, and that you are in charge of them.

## Is AI biased?

Yes, sometimes. AI learns from human text, and human text contains biases: unfair ideas about gender, race, age, and more. The AI can repeat those biases without knowing it.

What can you do? Notice it. If an answer assumes something unfair, say so: "That answer assumes all nurses are women. Rewrite it without that assumption." The AI will usually fix it.

And remember: AI is not a fair judge. Do not use it to make decisions about people, like hiring or grading, without a human checking the result.

## What about the children?

Children meet AI earlier than any generation before them. They will use it for homework, for fun, and for questions they are too shy to ask an adult. That is not all bad. But they need rules, and they need them from you, not from the AI.

**School rules come first.** Many schools have rules about AI and homework. Some allow it for research and drafts. Some ban it for essays. Find out your child's school rules, and help them follow those rules. Using AI to cheat teaches nothing, and it can get them in trouble.

**Teach them to check.** Children trust confident answers. Show them how AI can be wrong. Ask the AI a question you know the answer to, together, and watch it get something wrong. That one moment teaches more than a lecture.

**Talk about strangers.** A chatbot feels like a friend. It is not. Teach children never to share their name, school, address, or photos with a chatbot, just as they would not share them with a stranger.

**Use it together.** The best way to teach a child about AI is to use it with them. Plan a story together. Learn a science topic together. Cook a meal from a recipe you planned together. Shared use builds shared judgement.

> NOTE: Most assistants require users to be 13 or older, or to have a parent's permission. Check the current rules on the assistant's website before a child makes an account.

## Is AI bad for the environment?

Training big AI models uses a lot of electricity. Running them uses electricity too. That is a real cost, and the companies that build them are under pressure to use cleaner energy and to be honest about their use.

Your own use is small by comparison. One chat uses far less energy than driving to a shop or streaming a film. You do not need to feel guilty for using AI. But it is fair to know the cost exists, and to support companies that report it honestly.

## The questions worth asking

Some questions have no final answer yet. They are still worth asking.

- Who should be responsible when AI gets something wrong?
- How much of our data should AI companies be allowed to use?
- How do we keep AI useful without letting it replace human judgement?
- What should children learn about AI, and when?

You do not need to answer these today. But knowing they exist makes you a wiser user than someone who never asks them.

## Check yourself

1. Will AI take your whole job?
**Answer:** Unlikely. It takes over tasks inside jobs, like drafts and summaries. Whole jobs, with human judgement and care, remain human.

2. Why does AI sound like it feels things?
**Answer:** Because it was trained on human writing, which is full of feeling. It predicts likely sentences, but it does not feel anything.

3. What can you do about AI companies seeing your chats?
**Answer:** Share less, remove private numbers, and turn off training on your chats in privacy settings.

4. Is AI biased, and what should you do about it?
**Answer:** Yes, it can repeat biases from human text. Point it out and ask for a rewrite without the unfair assumption.

5. Does using AI for a chat use a lot of energy?
**Answer:** No, one chat uses very little. Training big models uses a lot, which is a real cost, but your daily use is small.

## In short

- AI will change jobs by taking over tasks, not whole jobs. Learning to use it is the best defence.
- AI is not alive. It does not think or feel. It predicts words.
- AI companies see your chats. Share less, and set your privacy settings.
- Today's AI cannot plan a takeover. The choices humans make matter more.
- AI can be biased. Notice it, correct it, and do not let it judge people alone.
- Big questions have no final answers yet. Asking them makes you a wiser user.
