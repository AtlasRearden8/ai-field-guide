# 5.5 Life admin and getting things done
<!-- original: ch. 95 -->

Life admin is the endless small jobs of running a household. Bills. Forms. Appointments. Lists. Chores. None of them is hard. Together, they eat your weekends. AI can carry the boring half. You keep the decisions.

Getting things done is not about working harder. It is about having a system you trust. When everything lives in your head, you forget things and feel stressed. When everything lives in a system, your head is free. AI makes that system easier to keep.

## The life-admin toolkit

You do not need ten apps. You need four tools and one habit.

Tool one is a notes app for capture. One inbox for every stray thought. A bill to pay. A form to fill. A birthday to remember. Capture takes ten seconds. If it takes longer, you will not do it.

Tool two is a calendar for time. Put every appointment and deadline in it. Set reminders. A task without a date is a wish. A task with a date is a plan.

Tool three is a task list for actions. Each task has one next step. "Call the dentist" is a task. "Sort out health" is not. Break big jobs into small steps. Small steps get done.

Tool four is an AI assistant for drafting and sorting. It drafts emails. It summarises long forms. It turns a messy list into a clean plan. That is its job. Your job is to check and decide.

The habit is the weekly review. Twenty minutes, once a week. Look at the inbox. Look at the calendar. Look at the task list. Ask what is done, what is stuck, and what can be dropped. AI can help by summarising the week. You make the calls.

> NOTE: Keep passwords, bank logins, and identity documents out of AI chats. AI is a helper, not a safe. Use a password manager for secrets.

## Five systems that run a household

System one is the Sunday planning session. Twenty minutes. Ask AI to look at your calendar and task list for the week ahead. Ask it three questions. What is the busiest day? What needs preparing? What can be moved? Then plan meals, chores, and appointments around the answers. A planned week feels lighter than a surprising one.

System two is the household command center. One page holds everything. The week's meals. The chores. The appointments. The shopping list. AI can draft the page each Sunday from your calendar and notes. You edit it. The family checks it. One page beats five group chats.

System three is the paperwork processor. Paperwork is forms, letters, and bills. Take a photo or scan. Ask AI to summarise it. What is it asking for? What is the deadline? What do I need to send? Then draft the reply or fill the form. Check every number and date before you send it. AI can misread a figure. You cannot afford that.

System four is the subscription audit. List every subscription you pay for. Ask AI to sort them by cost and last use. Cancel the ones you have not used in three months. Set a reminder to repeat the audit every three months. Most households find money hiding here.

System five is a personal CRM for the people you love. CRM stands for Customer Relationship Management. In plain words, it is a list of people and what matters to them. Birthdays. Favourite foods. The last time you called. AI can remind you before a birthday and suggest a message. You write the message yourself. People can tell the difference, and they care.

## Productivity methods, AI-enhanced

Classic methods still work. AI makes them easier.

Getting Things Done means capturing everything, then processing it into clear next steps. AI helps by turning a messy brain dump into a clean task list. Paste your rambling note. Ask for tasks with one next step each.

Time blocking means giving each job a slot in your calendar. Ask AI to draft your day from your task list. "I have three hours. These are my tasks. Plan the day." Then adjust it. You know your energy better than any model.

The Pomodoro method means working in short bursts. Twenty-five minutes of focus. Five minutes of rest. Repeat four times. Ask AI to be your timer and your checker. At each break, tell it what you finished. It keeps the tally and suggests the next step.

Eat the frog means doing the hardest job first. Ask AI to rank your tasks by difficulty and importance. Do the top one before anything else. The rest of the day feels easy after that.

## The overwhelm reset

Some days the list is too long and you freeze. That is normal. There is a reset for it.

Step one, stop. Close the list. Breathe. Step two, write down the three things that matter most today. Not ten. Three. Step three, ask AI to break the hardest one into tiny steps. Step four, do the tiniest step. One email. One call. One form field. Step five, stop for the day if you need to. A small win beats a frozen day.

Privacy matters in personal life. Do not paste medical letters, legal papers, or children's details into a public AI tool. Read the privacy settings. Delete chats you do not need. Keep family data in tools you trust.

A personal CRM sounds fancy. It is just a list of people and what matters to them. Birthdays. Favourite foods. The last time you called. The name of their dog. Keep it in your notes app. Ask AI to remind you before a birthday. Ask it to suggest a message. Then write the message yourself. People can tell the difference between your words and a robot's.
