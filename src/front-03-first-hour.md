# Your first hour: 60 minutes to your first win

You have one hour. By the end of it, you will have an AI assistant on your phone, you will have talked to it, and you will have something real to show for it. A plan, a draft, or an answer you actually needed.

Set a timer. Work through the steps. Do not skip ahead. Each step takes about ten minutes.

## Minutes 0 to 10: Pick your assistant

You need one assistant to start. Just one. You can change later, and it costs nothing to switch.

If you use an iPhone, start with ChatGPT or Claude. If you use Android or Gmail every day, start with Gemini. If you use Windows and Word all day, start with Copilot. If you truly cannot decide, start with ChatGPT. It is the most common choice, and the most help exists for it.

> NOTE: There is no wrong first assistant. They all do the same basic job. Picking one today beats researching all of them for a week.

## Minutes 10 to 20: Set it up

Go to your phone's app store. Search for the assistant's name. Check the developer name matches the real company: OpenAI for ChatGPT, Google for Gemini, Anthropic for Claude, Microsoft for Copilot. Install the app.

Open it. Make an account with your email. Choose a strong password, one you do not use anywhere else. Turn on two-step verification if it offers it. That means the app asks for a second code, usually from your phone, when you sign in somewhere new. It keeps your account safe.

Then tell it about you. Most assistants have a settings page with a box called "About me" or "Personalise". Write two or three sentences: your name, what you do, and what you like. For example: "I am Sam. I work in a school office. I like short, plain answers." This one small step makes every answer better.

## Minutes 20 to 35: Have your first chat

Now talk to it. Type your first message. Make it real, not a test. Ask about something you actually want.

Try this shape: say what you want, give some background, and say what you want back. For example:

"Plan three easy dinners for this week for two people. We like chicken and pasta. We do not like spicy food. Keep each meal under 30 minutes. Give me a shopping list too."

Read the answer. Then reply to it, the way you would reply to a person. Ask a follow-up: "Swap Tuesday for something vegetarian." Or: "Make the shopping list shorter." Notice how it remembers what you said. That is the whole trick. A chat is a conversation, not a search box.

> TRY IT: Ask it something only you would know the answer to. A fact about your town, your hobby, or your family. See if it gets it right. This teaches you the most important lesson in the book: check the answers that matter.

## Minutes 35 to 50: Try a second superpower

Typing is only one way in. Try one more.

- **Talk to it.** Tap the microphone button in the app. Speak your question out loud. Listen to the answer.
- **Show it a photo.** Take a picture of something: a plant, a letter, the inside of your fridge. Ask what it is, or what to do with it.
- **Give it a file.** Upload a photo of a bill or a letter. Ask it to explain the letter in plain English.

Pick the one that feels most useful to you. You do not need all three today.

## Minutes 50 to 60: Make something real

Finish with a win. Pick one small job from your real life and finish it with your assistant's help.

- A message you have been putting off. Ask for a draft, then edit it until it sounds like you.
- A plan for the weekend. Ask for ideas, then pick one.
- A confusing email. Paste it in and ask: "Explain this in plain words. What do I need to do?"

When it is done, save it. Copy the answer into your notes. Send the message. Print the plan. A finished job is the win. It proves the hour was worth it.

> NOTE: If something goes wrong in this hour, that is normal. A bad answer, a confusing screen, a login hiccup. Chapter 1.7 shows how to fix bad answers. Chapter 1.10 shows how to spot wrong ones. Nothing here can break your phone.

## Your first-hour checklist

- [ ] I picked one assistant and installed its app
- [ ] I made an account and turned on two-step verification
- [ ] I told it a little about me
- [ ] I had a real conversation, with at least one follow-up
- [ ] I tried voice, a photo, or a file
- [ ] I finished one real job and saved the result

Tick all six, and you are done. You are no longer a person who has never used AI. You are a person who uses AI.

Welcome to the field.
