# 2.13 Hubs and specialty chatbots
<!-- original: ch. 29 -->

Beyond the big-name assistants there is a whole ecosystem of clever AI apps. Hubs give you many models in one place. Privacy-first chatbots do not keep your data. Character apps let you talk to fictional people. Tutors teach. Specialists do one job very well.

This chapter maps the landscape. It points out the gems. It gives you a checklist for deciding whether a new AI app deserves your data and your money.

## Multi-model hubs: many AIs, one app

A **hub** is one app that offers many different AI models under one account, often one subscription. You can switch models with a tap. You can compare answers side by side. You can try a new model the day it launches.

Hubs are great for comparing answers and for trying new models without five subscriptions.

| Hub | What it is | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| Poe | A hub from Quora with many chat models in one app | Trying new models fast, comparing answers |
| HuggingChat | A free hub from Hugging Face, the open-model community | Open models, no cost, tinkering |
| OpenRouter | A hub that routes your request to many models | Builders, and trying models by price and speed |
| AI Revival | A hub with many models, including new releases | Trying fresh models like Inkling 2 Small |
| Perplexity Pro | A search hub that also offers top models | Research with a choice of model behind it |

**AI Revival** is one of the newer hubs. It gathers many models in one place, including new releases that have not yet reached the big apps. That is where you will find **Inkling 2 Small**, the thinking model from Thinking Machines Lab, released on November 5, 2026. On AI Revival it is listed under **Other models**, at **Light usage**. On the free plan you can use it 10 times a day. That is enough to try it on a real task. Do not claim more than that: prices and limits on hubs change often, so check the current terms in the app before you rely on them.

Developer hubs exist too. **Microsoft Azure AI Foundry** and **Amazon Bedrock** are model playgrounds aimed at developers. They are for building apps, not for everyday chat.

**Trade-offs.** Hubs may not have every feature of the original apps. Voice modes, memory, connectors, and agents are often missing or weaker. Your data also passes through two companies: the hub and the model provider. Both have their own privacy rules.

> NOTE: A hub is a shop, not a brand. The model you pick inside the hub is what matters. A great hub with a weak model still gives weak answers. Pick the model first, the hub second.

## Privacy-first assistants

Some assistants are built around privacy. They suit sensitive-but-everyday questions: health worries, personal situations, money. Questions where you would rather not build a profile anywhere.

| Assistant | What makes it private |
|---|---|
| Duck.ai | Chats are anonymized. DuckDuckGo does not tie them to you. |
| Proton Lumo | Zero-access encryption. Even the company cannot read your chats. |
| Brave Leo | Built into the Brave browser, with privacy controls. |
| Local AI | Run models on your own computer. Nothing leaves it. |

**Zero-access encryption** means your chats are scrambled with a key only you hold. The company stores the scrambled text. It cannot read it, even if it wanted to.

**Local AI** is the ultimate privacy option. Run a model on your own computer with a tool like Ollama. Nothing leaves your machine. There is a full guide later in this book, in the local AI chapters.

The trade-off is real. Privacy-first assistants usually have fewer bells and whistles. Fewer integrations, less memory, no fancy agents. You trade features for privacy. For many sensitive questions, that is a good trade.

**"Uncensored" does not mean "safe."** Some apps advertise "no filters." Be cautious. They may lack safeguards against harmful content. Some have weak privacy despite their marketing. Check who runs them and read reviews.

## Characters, companions, and tutors

**Characters and companions** are chatbots built to feel like people. Some are fictional characters. Some are companions designed for conversation.

- **Character.AI** lets you chat with characters, from historical figures to fictional heroes. Since late 2025, open-ended character chats are limited to adults, 18 and over. Teens get restricted story features.
- **Grok companions** are animated characters in the Grok app. See chapter 2.6.
- **Replika** and similar apps offer an AI friend you can talk to every day.
- **Pi** is a gentle, supportive chatbot built for conversation and thinking things through.

These apps can be fun, creative, and even comforting. But they are designed to be engaging, sometimes very engaging. Watch your time. Keep investing in human relationships. Never share secrets or money. Keep children and teens away from open-ended companion bots.

If you are struggling, reach out to real people and professional support. A chatbot is not a therapist.

**Learning and tutoring.** Specialist tutors often beat general assistants at teaching.

- **Khanmigo** is Khan Academy's tutor. It guides students step by step instead of giving answers.
- **Duolingo** uses AI for language practice, speaking, and feedback.
- **Speak** and similar apps give speaking practice for languages, with corrections.
- **Gemini Notebook** turns your own notes into quizzes, flashcards, and audio lessons. See chapter 2.3.

More on learning with AI lives later in this book, in the research and learning chapter.

> TRY IT: Ask the same personal-but-not-secret question, like "How can I sleep better when I am stressed?", in Duck.ai and in your main assistant. Notice the difference in features, and in how you feel about privacy. Then run one new AI app you have been curious about through the trust checklist below.

## Specialists for work and writing

Some tools do one job better than any general assistant. They are worth knowing about.

- **Writing.** Grammarly checks grammar and tone. Other writing tools help with drafts, rewrites, and style.
- **Meetings.** Otter and Fireflies record meetings, transcribe them, and write summaries. A **transcript** is a written record of everything that was said.
- **Notes.** Notion AI and Obsidian with AI turn notes into a second brain. They have full chapters later in this book.
- **Images and video.** Midjourney, Runway, and Kling go further than chatbot image tools. They are for people who make images and video seriously.
- **Coding.** Claude Code, Codex, and Cursor are coding agents. They build software with you. They have full chapters later in this book.

The rule is simple. Use a general assistant for everyday questions. Use a specialist when one job matters a lot and you do it often.

## Should you trust a new AI app? A checklist

Before signing up, and especially before paying or uploading personal data, run through this list.

- [ ] **Who makes it?** A known company with a real address, or an anonymous app?
- [ ] **What model does it use?** Many apps are "wrappers" around ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini. A **wrapper** is an app that puts a new interface on someone else's model. Is the extra value worth it?
- [ ] **Privacy policy.** Does it train on your data? Does it sell your data? Where is it stored? Can you delete it?
- [ ] **Price.** Watch for weekly subscriptions and hard-to-cancel trials. A weekly charge adds up fast.
- [ ] **Reviews.** What do independent reviewers and users say?
- [ ] **Lock-in.** Can you export your data if you leave?
- [ ] **Age and safety.** Is it appropriate for whoever will use it?

If an app fails two or more of these checks, skip it. There are plenty of good ones.

## Check yourself

1. You want to compare answers from several models without five subscriptions. What kind of tool?

**Answer:** A multi-model hub, like Poe, HuggingChat, OpenRouter, or AI Revival.

2. Which assistant uses encryption so even the company cannot read your chats?

**Answer:** Proton Lumo, with zero-access encryption. Duck.ai anonymizes chats. Local AI keeps everything on your device.

3. Your 14-year-old wants to use Character.AI to chat with characters. What is the situation?

**Answer:** Since late 2025, open-ended character chats are limited to adults, 18 and over. Teens get restricted story features. Talk with them about companion apps generally.

4. Where can you try Inkling 2 Small, and what is the free limit?

**Answer:** On the AI Revival hub, under Other models, at Light usage. On the free plan you can use it 10 times a day.

5. A new AI app looks fun but you know nothing about its maker. What should you check first?

**Answer:** Run the trust checklist: who makes it, what model it uses, its privacy policy, its price, reviews, and whether you can export your data.

## In short

- Hubs like Poe, HuggingChat, OpenRouter, and AI Revival give you many models in one place. Great for comparing.
- AI Revival is where you can try Inkling 2 Small: Light usage, 10 times a day on the free plan.
- Privacy-first assistants like Duck.ai, Proton Lumo, Brave Leo, and local AI suit sensitive questions.
- Companion and character apps can be fun but need boundaries. They are not for kids.
- Specialist tools for learning, writing, meetings, and creating often beat general assistants at one job.
- Use the trust checklist before handing any app your data or money.
