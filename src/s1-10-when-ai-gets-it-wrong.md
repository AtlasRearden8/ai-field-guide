# 1.10 When AI gets it wrong
<!-- original: ch. 10 -->

AI sometimes makes things up. It sounds confident anyway. This is the most important chapter in Section 1. Not the most fun. The most important. Because the better you get at spotting wrong answers, the more you can trust the right ones.

The technical word for AI making things up is **hallucination**. It means the AI produces something that sounds true but is not. A fake quote. A wrong date. A book that does not exist. A link that goes nowhere.

## Why it happens

Remember Chapter 1.2. The chatbot guesses the next word. It is not looking up facts in a book. It is predicting what a good answer would look like.

That works brilliantly for writing and ideas. It works badly for facts. When the AI does not know something, it does not stop. It guesses something that sounds right. The guess is smooth, detailed, and wrong.

It also has no sense of what it does not know. It cannot feel uncertain. So it never says "I have no idea" unless it has been trained to do so, and even then it often prefers a confident answer.

> NOTE: AI is most likely to be wrong about rare facts, recent events, numbers, names, quotes, and links. It is least likely to be wrong about general explanations and everyday advice.

## How to spot it

Wrong answers have tells. Learn to notice them.

**Broken or fake links.** The AI gives you a web address that goes nowhere, or to a page that has nothing to do with the topic. This is a classic sign. Always click links before you trust them.

**Too-perfect detail.** A quote that is suspiciously exact. A statistic with two decimal places. A book title you cannot find anywhere. Real facts are messy. Made-up facts are often too neat.

**Contradictions.** The answer says one thing in the first paragraph and the opposite in the third. The AI lost track.

**No sources.** The AI states facts but gives no source, or says "studies show" without naming a study. That is a warning light.

**It agrees with your mistake.** You put a wrong idea in your question. The AI repeats it back as fact. It is copying your pattern, not checking it.

## How bad is it? It depends

Not every wrong answer matters. Use this simple test: how bad is it if this is wrong?

| If it is wrong... | What to do |
|---|---|
| A story, a poem, a brainstorm | Enjoy it. "Right" does not apply. |
| A dinner plan, a gift idea | Low risk. Use your judgement. |
| A work email, a school fact | Check it once, from one good source. |
| Money, health, law, safety | Check it carefully, from two sources or a professional. |
| Something you will publish or send | Check every fact, quote, and link. |

The rule is simple. The higher the stakes, the harder you check.

## How to check facts

You do not need to become a detective. Four quick habits cover most cases.

1. **Ask for sources.** Say: "Give me your sources, with links." Then click the links. Do they exist? Do they say what the AI claims?
2. **Ask it to search the web.** If your assistant can search, say: "Search the web for this and check your answer." Fresh facts need fresh sources.
3. **Check with a second source.** A search engine, an official website, a trusted book. One check beats no check. Two checks beat one.
4. **Ask a different assistant.** Paste the same question into a second assistant. If both agree, you are safer. If they disagree, dig deeper.

> TRY IT: Ask your assistant: "What year was the first Moon landing, and who was the first person to walk on the Moon?" Then ask: "Give me a source for that." Click the source. It should check out. Now ask it something obscure about your town or your hobby, and check that answer the same way. Notice the difference in how easy each one is to verify.

## The fact-check ladder

Not every answer needs the same level of checking. Use this ladder. Climb as high as the stakes demand.

**Rung 1: The sniff test.** Does the answer feel right? Does it match what you already know? Does it contradict itself? Most everyday answers only need this rung. A dinner plan that suggests pineapple on a pizza is fine. A dinner plan that says to cook chicken for two minutes is not.

**Rung 2: The source check.** Ask for sources, with links. Click them. Do they exist? Do they say what the AI claims? This rung takes about a minute, and it catches most mistakes about facts, dates, and prices.

**Rung 3: The second opinion.** Check with another source: a search engine, an official website, or a different assistant. If two independent sources agree, you are much safer. Use this rung for work documents, schoolwork, and anything you will send to someone else.

**Rung 4: The expert check.** For money, health, law, and safety, ask a professional. An AI can help you prepare questions for a doctor, a lawyer, or an accountant. It cannot replace them. If the answer affects your health, your money, or your legal rights, get a human expert to confirm it.

Climb one rung at a time. Do not climb to rung 4 for a birthday poem. Do not stay on rung 1 for a medical question. Matching the check to the stakes is the skill.

## Make it show its working

You can reduce mistakes before they happen. Ask the AI to reason step by step.

- "Think step by step, then give me the answer."
- "Show your working for this maths problem."
- "List the facts you are sure about, and the ones you are guessing."

For maths, ask it to check its own sums. For plans, ask it to list what could go wrong. For quotes, ask where the quote comes from.

None of this makes AI perfect. It makes it more careful, and it makes its mistakes easier to spot.

## What to do when you catch it

When you catch a wrong answer, do three things.

1. **Tell the AI.** "That is wrong. The correct date is..." It will correct itself in this chat.
2. **Do not copy the wrong answer elsewhere.** Delete it, or mark it as wrong.
3. **Remember the pattern.** If it keeps getting a topic wrong, that topic needs checking every time.

Over time you will learn which topics your assistant handles well and which it fumbles. That knowledge is worth more than any single fact.

## Check yourself

1. What is a hallucination, in plain words?
**Answer:** When the AI produces something that sounds true but is not, like a fake quote, a wrong date, or a link that goes nowhere.

2. Why does AI make things up instead of saying "I do not know"?
**Answer:** Because it guesses the next word, and a smooth, confident answer is more likely than admitting it does not know.

3. Name three warning signs of a wrong answer.
**Answer:** Broken or fake links, too-perfect detail, contradictions, no sources, or agreeing with a mistake in your question.

4. How do you decide how hard to check an answer?
**Answer:** By the stakes. Low stakes need little checking. Money, health, law, and safety need careful checking from two sources or a professional.

5. What four habits help you check facts?
**Answer:** Ask for sources and click them, ask it to search the web, check with a second source, and ask a different assistant.

## In short

- AI makes things up. It sounds confident anyway. That is called hallucination.
- It happens because AI guesses the next word, not because it looks up facts.
- Watch for fake links, too-perfect detail, contradictions, and missing sources.
- The higher the stakes, the harder you check.
- Ask for sources, search the web, and get a second opinion.
- Catching mistakes makes you a better user, not a worse one.
