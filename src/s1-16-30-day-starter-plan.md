# 1.16 Your 30-day starter plan
<!-- original: ch. 16 -->

Thirty days. About ten minutes a day. That is all it takes to go from "I have tried it" to "I use it without thinking." This plan builds the habits that make AI genuinely useful: a little practice every day, a new skill each week, and a clear map of where to go next when the month is over.

Print this chapter. Stick it on the fridge. Tick the boxes.

## How the plan works

About ten minutes a day. Some days take five. A few take twenty. Missed a day? No guilt. Just pick up where you left off.

Keep a tiny log: one line a day in your notes. "Day 6: AI fixed my Wi-Fi!" It is motivating to look back on.

Do it with a buddy if you can: a partner, a friend, or a parent. Compare notes each week.

Any assistant works. Where a day mentions a feature, your assistant's own help pages will show you where to find it.

> NOTE: The goal is not to finish perfectly. The goal is to finish with a habit. Ten minutes a day, most days, beats two heroic weekends.

## Week 1: The basics

Days 1 to 7. Meet your assistant and learn to ask well. If you did Chapter 1.15, your first week, you have already done most of this. Do it again, faster, or skip to Week 2.

| Day | Do this |
|---|---|
| 1 | Pick your assistant, install the app, and make an account |
| 2 | Fill in "About me" and turn on two-step verification |
| 3 | Have your first real chat, with a follow-up |
| 4 | Try voice mode for one question |
| 5 | Upload a photo and ask about it |
| 6 | Ask for a meal plan or a weekend plan, with details |
| 7 | Save your three best prompts in a note called "My prompts" |

## Week 2: Everyday habits

Days 8 to 14. Use AI for real jobs, every day.

| Day | Do this |
|---|---|
| 8 | Draft a real email with AI, edit it, and send it |
| 9 | Summarise a long email or article in five bullets |
| 10 | Ask AI to explain a confusing letter or bill in plain English |
| 11 | Plan something real: a meal, a trip, or a weekend |
| 12 | Learn something new in 20 minutes, with AI as your tutor |
| 13 | Make something fun: a poem, a story, or an image |
| 14 | Teach a friend or family member one AI trick you learned |

## Week 3: Level up

Days 15 to 21. New tools, new tricks, and checking facts like a pro.

| Day | Do this |
|---|---|
| 15 | Explore your assistant's help pages and try two features you did not know about |
| 16 | Ask AI to search the web and check its own answer with sources |
| 17 | Run the same task through two different assistants and compare them |
| 18 | Upload a file and ask questions "only from this document" |
| 19 | Ask AI to be brutally honest about a plan or a draft |
| 20 | Practise a conversation in role-play: an interview, a negotiation, or a language lesson |
| 21 | Review your log. Write down your three biggest wins so far |

## Week 4: Advanced beginner

Days 22 to 30. Build helpers, connect apps, and choose your next step.

| Day | Do this |
|---|---|
| 22 | Build a reusable helper (a Project, Gem, Space, or saved prompt) for a job you do often |
| 23 | Connect one app, like Gmail, Calendar, or Drive, and ask about your own stuff |
| 24 | Have AI critique your work honestly, then improve it |
| 25 | Try something creative and new: a song, a short video, a story, or a podcast from your notes |
| 26 | Use AI to plan a real project, step by step, with dates |
| 27 | Teach someone one AI trick you have learned |
| 28 | Read Chapter 1.14, Myths, Fears, and Big Questions, and discuss one with someone |
| 29 | Pick your path from the map below |
| 30 | Celebrate! Look back at your log and write down your top three wins |

> TRY IT: Right now, do Day 1 in your head. Which assistant will you pick? When will you do your ten minutes today? A plan with a time is a plan that happens. Write it down.

## If you fall behind

Life happens. You will miss days. You may miss a whole week. That is normal, and it is not a failure.

The plan has a built-in fix: just carry on where you left off. Do not try to catch up by doing three days in one. That turns a habit into a chore. Do today's task, or the next one you missed, and move on.

If you fall behind by more than a week, restart the current week. Do not restart the whole plan. You have already learned the earlier skills. They are still yours. Repeat the week you are on, and carry on.

If you stop completely for a month, start again at Week 1, but move fast. You will find it much easier the second time. The skills come back quickly. The second attempt is often the one that sticks, because you know what to expect.

A buddy helps here too. Tell your buddy when you fall behind. Ask them what they did that week. A short chat about it is often enough to get you going again. The plan works best when it is shared.

> TRY IT: Write down your restart rule now, in your log: "If I miss a day, I carry on. If I miss a week, I repeat the week. If I stop for a month, I start at Week 1, fast." A rule decided in advance is easier to follow than a decision made when you feel guilty.

## Signs you are ready for more

You are an advanced beginner when:

- You use AI most days without having to think about it.
- You naturally add context and steer answers.
- You fact-check anything important out of habit.
- You have built at least one reusable helper.
- You catch yourself thinking: "I wonder if AI could connect to my calendar, automate this, or build me an app..."

That last one is the sign. The rest of this book is all about answering "yes, it can, and here is how."

## Check yourself

1. You missed three days of the plan. What should you do?
**Answer:** Just carry on where you left off. No guilt. Consistency beats perfection.

2. What are the four weekly themes of the plan?
**Answer:** Week 1, the basics. Week 2, everyday habits. Week 3, level up. Week 4, advanced beginner.

3. Why should you keep a one-line log?
**Answer:** Because looking back at your wins is motivating, and it shows you how far you have come.

4. Which section helps you master your specific assistant?
**Answer:** Section 2, The Workshop, which has a complete chapter for each major assistant.

5. What is the clearest sign you are ready for the advanced parts?
**Answer:** When you start wondering whether AI could connect to your apps, automate chores, or build things.

## In short

- Ten minutes a day for 30 days builds lasting AI habits.
- Each week adds a layer: basics, everyday habits, new tricks, helpers and connections.
- Missed a day? Carry on. No guilt.
- Keep a one-line log and do it with a buddy if you can.
- When you finish, pick a path: assistants, foundations, automation, building, creativity, or real life.
- The sign you are ready is curiosity about what AI could do next.
