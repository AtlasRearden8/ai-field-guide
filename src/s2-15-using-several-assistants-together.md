# 2.15 Using several assistants together
<!-- original: ch. 31 -->

You do not have to pick just one. Plenty of savvy users keep a main assistant plus one or two specialists. They pass work between them. They use them to double-check each other.

This chapter shows you how to build your personal AI squad. You will learn the best hand-off workflows, how to carry your preferences and memory between apps, and how to do it all without spreading your data everywhere or paying for five subscriptions.

## Why use more than one?

There are four good reasons.

**Strengths differ.** Sources belong to Perplexity. Google life belongs to Gemini. Microsoft work belongs to Copilot. Writing and documents belong to Claude. Images and all-round features belong to ChatGPT. Chats you are already in belong to Meta AI. No single assistant wins at everything.

**Second opinions catch errors.** When two independent assistants agree, you can be more confident. When they disagree, you know to dig deeper. Two AIs agreeing is not proof, since they learned from similar data. For important topics, still check official sources.

**Limits.** Hit the free limit on one assistant? Carry on in another. Free tiers are generous if you spread the load.

**Staying current.** New models land every month. Trying them keeps your skills fresh. That is how you meet a new model like Inkling 2 Small: not by replacing your main assistant, but by adding it as a specialist for long, careful jobs.

> NOTE: More assistants is not always better. If juggling apps costs more time than it saves, simplify back to one main assistant. Two is the sweet spot for most people. Three is the most anyone needs.

## Build your AI squad

Most people need **one main assistant plus one or two specialists**. Pick your main assistant first. It is the one you open every day. Then add specialists for the jobs your main one handles badly.

Here are some sample squads.

| Squad | Main | Specialists | Why it works |
|---|---|---|---|
| The writer | Claude | Perplexity, ChatGPT | Writing, sources, images |
| The Google household | Gemini | Perplexity, Claude | Your data, sources, long documents |
| The Microsoft worker | Copilot | ChatGPT, Perplexity | Office work, all-round chat, sources |
| The researcher | Perplexity | Gemini, Claude | Sources first, then your data, then writing |
| The builder | Claude or ChatGPT | Gemini, Perplexity | Artifacts or Codex, AI Studio, sources |
| The budget watcher | ChatGPT free | DeepSeek, Mistral Vibe | Free tiers, reasoning, speed |

A **squad** is just your set of assistants. Give it a name if you like. The point is to choose on purpose, not to collect apps at random.

## Hand-off workflows

The magic of a squad is **relay work**. Each assistant does what it is best at. Then you pass the result to the next one.

Picture a relay race. One runner runs the first leg. The next runner takes the baton. Nobody runs the whole race alone.

Ready-made relays:

| Goal | Relay |
|---|---|
| Blog post or newsletter | Perplexity finds and cites facts, then Claude or ChatGPT writes the article, then an image assistant makes the header, then you fact-check |
| Big buy | Perplexity makes a sourced comparison, then your main assistant advises: "Given my situation, which should I pick?" |
| Trip | Gemini reads bookings from Gmail and Maps, then ChatGPT or Claude makes a day-by-day plan, then Meta AI shares it in the group chat |
| Presentation | Claude writes the outline and speaker notes, then Copilot in PowerPoint or Gemini in Slides builds the deck |
| Learning | Gemini Notebook makes a podcast from your sources, then any assistant in voice mode quizzes you on a walk |
| Tricky problem | Inkling 2 Small thinks it through step by step, then your main assistant turns its answer into plain words |

When you hand work off, paste the previous result and say what you want next. Try: "Here is research from another AI. Check it for gaps, then write a 600-word article from it."

That one sentence is the whole trick. Paste the result. Say what is next. Done.

> TRY IT: Try one relay today. Research something in Perplexity, write it up in your main assistant, and have a third assistant create an image for it. You just directed an AI team.

## The second opinion and judge patterns

These are two simple habits that catch errors.

**Second opinion.** Ask two assistants the same important question, independently. Compare. Agreement builds confidence. Disagreement tells you where to check. Use this for money, health, and legal questions. Always take the result to a human expert for the final call.

**Judge.** Paste one assistant's answer into another. Say: "Here is an answer from another AI. What is wrong, missing, or overconfident? Be specific." This catches a surprising amount. The judge has no reason to defend the first answer.

**Debate.** Say: "Assistant A recommends a Roth IRA and assistant B recommends paying off debt first. Lay out the strongest case for each, and what would decide it." A **Roth IRA** is a type of retirement savings account in the US. The debate pattern works for any either-or choice.

Remember: two AIs agreeing is not proof. They learned from similar data. They can share the same blind spots. Real sources still matter.

## Take your "about me" everywhere

Keep a **portable profile**. It is a note you can paste into any assistant's custom instructions. Write it once. Use it everywhere.

Here is an example. Make your own version.

- Name: Alex. Lives: Leeds, UK. Family: partner and 2 kids, ages 8 and 11, dog Biscuit.
- Work: school administrator. Not very technical.
- Food: mostly vegetarian. No mushrooms.
- How I like answers: warm, brief, bullet points. British spelling, metric units.
- Flag anything I should double-check: money, health, legal.
- Ask me a question if my request is unclear.

**Moving memory between assistants** is easy too.

1. In your old assistant, ask: "Summarize everything you know about me and my preferences, as a list I can paste into another assistant. Leave out anything sensitive."
2. Review the list. Delete anything too personal.
3. Paste it into the new assistant's custom instructions or memory.
4. Some apps offer direct imports. Mistral Vibe can import ChatGPT memories in one click.

Keep your favorite prompts in the same note. Then your prompt library goes wherever you go.

## Exporting your chats and data

Most assistants let you **download your data**. Look in Settings, under Data controls, Privacy, or Account. Exporting is useful when you switch assistants. It is also a good habit for important projects.

| Assistant | Where to export |
|---|---|
| ChatGPT | Settings, then Data controls, then Export data |
| Gemini | Google Takeout, or Gemini Apps Activity |
| Claude | Settings, then Privacy, then Download your data |
| Copilot | Microsoft account privacy dashboard |
| Perplexity, Grok, Vibe | Settings, then account or data controls |

Exporting gives you a file with your chats. Keep it somewhere safe. It is your work, and you should own a copy.

## Smart subscription strategy

Pay for one plan. At most two. Your main assistant is where extra limits pay off. Use free tiers for specialists and second opinions.

- **Rotate.** Some people switch their one paid plan every few months to try the newest features.
- **Check bundles.** Google AI plans include storage. Microsoft 365 includes Office apps. Prime includes Alexa+. Phone deals may already cover you. You may be paying for something you already own.
- **Hubs.** Poe or Perplexity Pro give access to several models for one price. See chapter 2.13.
- **Review monthly.** Forgotten subscriptions are the classic money leak. Put a monthly reminder in your calendar. Cancel what you do not use.

A paid plan costs about $20 a month. Two cost about $40. Five cost about $100. Ask yourself what the second, third, and fourth plans actually add. Usually the answer is: not much.

## Pitfalls to avoid

**Data sprawl.** Every assistant you use holds some of your data. Keep sensitive topics to one trusted assistant, or to local AI. Delete what you do not need.

**Too many tools.** If juggling apps costs more time than it saves, simplify. One main assistant, done well, beats five used badly.

**Echo chambers.** Assistants trained on similar data can share the same blind spots. They can agree with each other and still be wrong. Real sources still matter.

**Forgotten subscriptions.** Review them monthly. A subscription you forgot is money you gave away.

**Copying answers without checking.** A relay is only as good as its last check. You are the editor. Read the final result before you send it, publish it, or act on it.

## Check yourself

1. What is the "judge" pattern?

**Answer:** Paste one assistant's answer into another and ask it to find what is wrong, missing, or overconfident.

2. How do you move your preferences from ChatGPT to a new assistant?

**Answer:** Ask ChatGPT to summarize what it knows about you, review it, and paste it into the new assistant's custom instructions or memory. Or use a direct import where offered, like Vibe's.

3. Should you pay for three assistants?

**Answer:** Usually not. Pay for one, maybe two, and use free tiers for the rest.

4. What is a relay, and what is one example?

**Answer:** A relay passes work between assistants, each doing what it is best at. For example: Perplexity researches with sources, Claude writes the article, and an image assistant makes the header.

5. What is data sprawl, and how do you avoid it?

**Answer:** Data sprawl is your information spread across many assistants. Avoid it by keeping sensitive topics to one trusted assistant and deleting what you do not need.

## In short

- A main assistant plus one or two specialists covers almost everyone.
- Relay work between assistants to use each one's strengths.
- Use second opinions and judges to catch errors.
- Keep a portable "about me" note and prompt library to move between assistants easily.
- Export your data. Pay for one plan, at most two.
- Avoid data sprawl, too many tools, and forgotten subscriptions.
