# 3.2 Obsidian and AI: your local thinking machine
<!-- original: ch. 56 -->

Obsidian is a notes app with one big difference. Your notes live on your computer as plain files. They are not locked inside a company's cloud. You own them. You can open them with any text editor. You can back them up yourself. Add AI, and those local files become a thinking machine you control.

Obsidian stores notes in Markdown. Markdown is a plain-text way to write formatted notes. A heading is a line that starts with "#". A link to another note uses double square brackets, like [[my note]]. That is almost all you need to know to start.

A vault is a folder full of notes. That folder is your Obsidian vault. Everything in it is yours. If Obsidian vanished tomorrow, your notes would still be there, readable in any app.

## Why Obsidian plus AI is special

Most AI notes tools send your notes to a server. Obsidian does not have to. You choose how AI reaches your notes. That choice is the whole point.

You get three things at once. Your notes are private by default. Your notes link together into a web. And AI can read that web and answer questions about it. People call that web a graph. The graph view shows your notes as dots and their links as lines. It looks like a map of your mind.

Start with one vault. Keep it simple. Make folders for a few areas of your life. Work. Personal. Learning. That is enough. Inside each folder, notes link to each other. A book note links to the project it inspired. A daily note links to the people you met.

> NOTE: Obsidian itself is free for personal use. Some AI plugins are free. Some charge a fee. Some need a paid AI account. Check the current price of any plugin before you install it.

The daily note is the heart of the system. Each day gets one note. You write what happened, what you are thinking, and what you need to do. Over weeks, daily notes become a diary you can search. AI can read them and spot patterns you would miss.

## Three ways to add AI

There are three main routes. Pick one to start. You can add the others later.

Route one is in-app plugins. Community plugins are small add-ons built by other users. Two well-known ones help with AI. Copilot connects your vault to an AI chat. Smart Connections finds notes related to the one you are reading. Install a plugin from the settings menu. Then open its panel and ask it a question about your notes. This is the easiest route.

Route two is MCP. MCP is a standard plug that lets an AI assistant use your tools. You can read about it in Section 2. An Obsidian MCP server lets Claude or ChatGPT read and search your vault. You ask the assistant a question in its own app. It looks in your vault and answers. This route is powerful. It also needs care. Only give vault access to an assistant you trust. Keep sensitive notes out of any vault the AI can read.

Route three is a coding agent in your vault. A coding agent is an AI that can read and edit files. Tools like Claude Code can work directly in your vault folder. You can ask it to reorganise notes, fix links, or build a summary page. This route is for confident users. It can change many files at once. Always back up your vault first.

> TRY IT: Install Obsidian and create a vault called "Field notes". Write three short notes: one about a book, one about a project, and one daily note. Link them together with [[double brackets]]. Open the graph view and watch the three dots connect.

## Workflows that stick

Tools do not make a system. Habits do. Here are three workflows that actually stick.

The AI-assisted daily note. Each morning, open today's note. Write three lines. What matters today? What am I worried about? What is one small win from yesterday? In the evening, add two more lines. What happened? What did I learn? Once a week, ask the AI to read the week's daily notes and summarise the themes.

The capture pipeline. Ideas arrive at bad times. Capture them fast. On your phone, write the idea into a note called "Inbox". Do not organise it yet. Once a day, process the inbox. Each item gets one of three fates. It becomes a real note. It becomes a task. Or it gets deleted. An inbox with fifty old items is not a pipeline. It is a guilt pile.

The weekly review. Ask the AI to read your notes from the week. Ask it four questions. What did I finish? What is still open? What did I learn? What should I focus on next week? Save the answers as a new note. Link it to the daily notes it mentions.

Here are a few more things to ask an AI with vault access. Summarise this long note in five bullets. What notes do I have about this topic? Find contradictions between these two notes. Turn this rough idea into an outline. What have I not looked at in a month? Draft a weekly review from my daily notes.

## Privacy, sync, and your safety net

Private mode means fully local AI. You can run a small AI model on your own computer. It reads your vault without sending anything to the internet. This is slower than cloud AI. It is also the most private option. It is a good fit for journals, health notes, or work secrets. Local models are covered in Section 2.

Syncing across devices needs a plan. Obsidian Sync is the official paid option. It is simple and it works. Other options exist, like storing your vault in a cloud folder. Whatever you pick, test it. Edit a note on your phone. Check that the change appears on your computer. A sync you have not tested is a sync you cannot trust.

Your safety net is a backup. Copy your vault folder to a second place once a week. A USB drive works. A cloud folder works. Your vault is just files. Backing it up is just copying a folder. Do it before you try any new plugin or agent that edits many files.

> NOTE: Community plugins are built by volunteers, not by Obsidian. Read the plugin's reviews and check when it was last updated before you install it. Remove any plugin you do not use.

Syncing deserves a test before you trust it. Edit a note on your phone. Wait a minute. Check your computer. Did the change appear? Now edit on the computer and check the phone. If both directions work, your sync is real. If one direction fails, fix it before you add more notes. A sync that loses notes is worse than no sync at all.

Your safety net is simple. Copy the vault folder to a second place once a week. A USB drive works. A cloud folder works. The copy takes a minute. It saves you from a broken plugin, a bad agent run, or a lost laptop. Keep two copies if the notes matter to you. One copy is luck. Two copies is a backup.

Here are more things to ask an AI with vault access. Find every note that mentions this person. What did I write about this book? Summarise my notes on this project into one page. Which ideas have I not touched in a month? Turn these five rough notes into one clean page. Each question turns a pile of files into a thinking partner.

## Check yourself

1. What is a vault in Obsidian?
**Answer:** A folder on your computer that holds all your notes as plain Markdown files.

2. Why do people say Obsidian is local-first?
**Answer:** Your notes live on your own computer as files you own, instead of living only in a company's cloud.

3. What are the three ways to add AI to Obsidian?
**Answer:** In-app community plugins, an MCP server that lets an assistant read your vault, and a coding agent working directly in the vault folder.

4. What is the capture pipeline?
**Answer:** A habit of dropping ideas fast into an Inbox note, then processing each item daily into a real note, a task, or the bin.

5. Why back up your vault before using a coding agent?
**Answer:** Because a coding agent can change many files at once, and a backup lets you undo any mistake.

## In short

- Obsidian keeps your notes as plain files on your computer, in a vault you own.
- Notes link together with [[double brackets]] and form a graph you can explore.
- AI can join through plugins, an MCP server, or a coding agent in the vault.
- Daily notes, a capture inbox, and a weekly review are the habits that make it work.
- Fully local AI is the most private option, and it is slower than cloud AI.
- Back up your vault folder every week, and test your sync before you trust it.
