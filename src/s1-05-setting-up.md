# 1.5 Setting up: accounts, apps, and plans
<!-- original: ch. 05 -->

A few minutes of setup makes every future chat better. It also makes your account safer and your answers more personal. This chapter walks through each step, in order. Set aside ten minutes and a cup of tea.

## Phone, computer, or browser?

Your account works everywhere. Your chats sync between devices. Most people use two of these.

**Phone app.** Best for quick questions, voice chats on a walk, and snapping photos. Get it from your phone's app store. Search for the assistant's name. Check the developer name matches the real company: OpenAI for ChatGPT, Google for Gemini, Anthropic for Claude, Microsoft for Copilot. Fake apps exist. The real one is free to install.

**Computer app.** Best for long writing and working with files. Most assistants offer a desktop app for Mac and Windows. Download it only from the assistant's own website, never from a random link.

**Browser.** Best when you are on someone else's computer, or you just want to try it fast. Go to the assistant's website and sign in. No install needed. Your chats still save to your account.

> NOTE: If an app asks you to pay just to install it, stop. The real apps are free to install. You only ever pay for a monthly plan, inside the real app or on the real website.

## Lock the door: account security

Your AI account will hold your chats. Some of those chats will be personal. Lock the door properly.

1. **Use a strong, unique password.** One you do not use anywhere else. A password manager can make one for you and remember it.
2. **Turn on two-step verification.** This is also called two-factor authentication, or 2FA. It means that when you sign in somewhere new, the app asks for a second code, usually sent to your phone or from an authenticator app. Even if someone learns your password, they cannot get in without that code.
3. **Sign in with Google or Apple if you prefer.** Many assistants let you sign in with your Google or Apple account instead of a new password. That is fine, and it is one less password to remember.
4. **Check where you are signed in.** Once a month, look at the security settings. Sign out of devices you no longer use.

> TRY IT: Right now, open your assistant's settings and check whether two-step verification is on. If it is off, turn it on. It takes two minutes, and it is the single best security step you can take.

## Five settings to check on day one

Open settings. Look for these five. They are in different places in each app, but they all exist.

1. **Your name and "About me".** Write two or three sentences about yourself. Your name, what you do, how you like answers. Short, plain, and direct works best. This one box improves every answer you will ever get.
2. **How it should talk to you.** Some apps let you set a tone: short, detailed, formal, friendly. Pick "short and plain" to start. You can change it any time.
3. **Memory.** Some assistants remember facts about you across chats, like your job or your preferences. Turn it on if you want a more personal helper. Turn it off if you prefer a clean slate each time. Either is fine.
4. **Training on your chats.** Some companies use your chats to improve their models. Most let you turn this off in privacy settings. If that bothers you, turn it off. Your assistant still works the same.
5. **Voice.** Pick a voice you like, if your app offers voice chat. Try two or three. You will be listening to this voice a lot.

## Tell it about you

This deserves its own section, because it makes such a big difference. The assistant knows nothing about you until you tell it. A few sentences change everything.

Write something like this in the "About me" box:

"I am Priya. I work in a school office. I have two children, aged 7 and 10. I like short answers in plain English. I am vegetarian. I live in Leeds."

Now the assistant can plan vegetarian dinners, explain things simply, and write school emails in the right tone. Without that box, it guesses. With it, it knows.

Update the box when your life changes. A new job, a new hobby, a new city. Five minutes, twice a year, keeps it fresh.

## Free vs. paid plans, in plain English

Every major assistant has a free plan and at least one paid plan. Here is what that means, without the sales talk.

**Free plan.** You get the assistant, the app, and a good model. You get a limited number of messages per day, or per few hours, with the newest models. You may not get the fanciest tools. For learning and light daily use, free is enough.

**Paid plan.** You pay every month. In return you get more messages, the newest and smartest models, faster answers, bigger file uploads, and extra tools like deeper research or image generation. Check the current price on the assistant's own website before you pay, because prices change and differ by country.

**Team or family plans.** Some assistants offer plans for groups. They cost more. They suit a small business or a family that shares. You do not need one to learn.

How do you decide? Use the free plan for two weeks. If you keep hitting limits, or you keep wanting a feature you cannot use, try one month of paid. One month. Then judge it. You can cancel any time.

> NOTE: Watch out for surprise costs. A monthly plan is a subscription, which means it renews by itself each month. Put a reminder in your calendar to check it after a month. If you are not using it, cancel it.

## When setup goes wrong

Setup is usually smooth. When it is not, here is what to do about the common problems.

**"I cannot sign in."** Check your email address for a typo. Check your password. Use "forgot password" to reset it. If you signed up with Google or Apple, use that button instead of an email and password. Do not make a second account. One account, fixed, is better than two.

**"The app will not install."** Check you have space on your phone. Check your phone's software is up to date. Try the browser version instead. It works the same, with no install.

**"It says I have hit a limit."** Free plans limit how many messages you can send in a few hours, especially with the newest models. Wait an hour, or switch to an older model in the app's model picker. You do not need to pay. You need to wait.

**"It keeps answering in the wrong language."** Tell it, in your language: "Please always answer in English." Then check the language setting in the app. One clear instruction usually fixes it.

**"I do not like the answers."** Fill in "About me" properly, with two or three sentences about yourself. Then give better prompts: task, context, details, format, tone. Most bad answers are caused by thin prompts, not by a bad assistant.

> NOTE: If a problem asks you to pay money, download a file, or share a code, stop. Go to the assistant's official help pages instead. Real support never asks for your password or a payment to fix a login.

## Families, kids, and shared devices

If children use your phone or computer, think about this now.

- **Use separate accounts where possible.** A child's chats should not mix with yours.
- **Turn off memory and training** on a shared device, or use a guest session.
- **Talk to children about what not to share.** Names, addresses, school names, and photos of other people do not belong in a chatbot. Chapter 1.11 covers this in full.
- **Check the assistant's age rules.** Most assistants require users to be 13 or older, or to have a parent's permission. Check the current rules on the assistant's website.

- [ ] Installed the official app on my phone, computer, or both
- [ ] Made an account with a strong, unique password
- [ ] Turned on two-step verification
- [ ] Filled in "About me" with two or three sentences
- [ ] Set the tone to short and plain
- [ ] Checked the memory and training settings
- [ ] Picked the free plan to start

Tick them all, and your setup is done. You will not need to think about it again for months.

## Check yourself

1. How do you know you are installing the real app, not a fake?
**Answer:** Check the developer name matches the real company, and get it from the official app store or the assistant's own website.

2. What is two-step verification, and why does it matter?
**Answer:** It asks for a second code, usually from your phone, when you sign in somewhere new. Even if someone learns your password, they cannot get in.

3. What should you write in the "About me" box?
**Answer:** Two or three sentences: your name, what you do, and how you like answers. It makes every reply fit your life better.

4. What does a paid plan give you that a free plan does not?
**Answer:** More messages, the newest models, faster answers, bigger files, and extra tools. Check the current price before you pay.

5. What is the danger of a monthly subscription?
**Answer:** It renews by itself each month. Set a reminder to check it, and cancel it if you are not using it.

## In short

- Install the real app, from the real store or website.
- Lock your account with a unique password and two-step verification.
- Fill in "About me". It improves every answer.
- Check five settings on day one: name, tone, memory, training, voice.
- Start free. Pay only after two weeks of real use, if you hit limits.
- On a shared device, keep accounts separate and talk to kids about sharing.
