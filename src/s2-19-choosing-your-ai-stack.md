# 2.19 Choosing your AI stack
<!-- original: ch. 37 -->

There are thousands of AI tools. You need maybe five. This chapter helps you build a small, powerful personal AI stack that fits your goals, habits, and budget. You will leave with a filled-in worksheet and a clear idea of what to try next.

Your **stack** is the set of tools you use every day. Choosing it on purpose saves money and keeps life simple. Choosing it by accident leads to five subscriptions, three half-used apps, and a bill you cannot explain.

## The five layers

A good stack has five layers. You do not need a tool for every layer on day one. But knowing the layers stops you buying two tools that do the same job.

| Layer | Job | How many you need |
|---|---|---|
| Main assistant | Daily thinking partner, research, writing | One paid plan, maybe two |
| Connections | Let the assistant see and act on your stuff | A handful of connectors |
| Automation | Things that run without you | One platform |
| Knowledge hub | Where your information lives | One home base |
| Builder tools | Make your own tools and apps | Optional, and the most fun |

Start with the main assistant. Pick by where you already live and what you do most. If you write a lot, code, or want deep thinking, lean toward Claude. If you want the broadest all-rounder with voice and images, lean toward ChatGPT. If you live in Gmail, Docs, Drive, or Android, lean toward Gemini. If you live in Outlook, Teams, or Office, lean toward Microsoft 365 Copilot. If you mostly research with citations, lean toward Perplexity. If privacy matters most, lean toward local models.

A handy trick: keep one paid main assistant and use a second one's free tier for second opinions. When two models agree, confidence goes up. When they disagree, you have found the tricky part.

> NOTE: The person who masters one tool beats the person who samples twenty. Learn deeply before you switch.

## Subscriptions versus API: the money question

There are two ways to pay for AI. A **subscription** is a flat monthly fee, like a gym membership. An **API** charges per token, so you pay for exactly what you use. Each suits different jobs.

| | Subscription | API (pay per token) |
|---|---|---|
| Pricing | Flat monthly | Pay for exactly what you use |
| Best for | Chatting, research, coding you drive yourself | Automations, your own apps, scripts, bots |
| Surprise bills? | Never; you hit usage limits instead | Possible, so set spend limits |
| Needed for | Using the apps | Automation steps and custom agents |

Most people end up using both. A subscription for daily life. An API key with a low monthly spend limit for automations. Small automations often cost cents per day. The full money-saving playbook is in chapter 2.38.

> TRY IT: List every AI subscription you pay for. Write the price next to each. Circle any you have not opened in 30 days. That circle is money you can save today.

## Starter stacks by persona

Different lives need different stacks. Find the one closest to yours and use it as a starting point.

**The student.** A free tier, or a student discount on one paid plan. Add a notebook tool for studying and an app for flashcards. Keep it cheap. Your time is the budget.

**The busy household.** One paid plan, used carefully, or free tiers. Connect the family calendar and shared notes. Automate with phone shortcuts or home routines. Add a meal-planning project and a homework helper.

**The productivity maximiser.** One paid assistant, plus email, calendar, Drive or SharePoint, and Slack connected. One automation platform. One knowledge hub, like Notion. Add a meeting notetaker and a dictation app.

**The builder.** A paid assistant with a coding agent, plus code and database connectors. A self-hosted automation tool. API keys with spend limits. Obsidian or Notion plus code repos. Free hosting tiers for projects.

**The privacy-first tinkerer.** Local models at home with Ollama and Open WebUI. A frontier model only for non-sensitive hard problems. Self-hosted automation pointed at local models. Local notes. Local transcription.

**The creator.** A paid assistant, plus an image tool and a design tool. A video model, a voice tool, and a music tool. An automation platform for repurposing content across channels.

**The small business owner.** A team plan with admin controls. CRM, email, calendar, and accounting connected. Automation with human-approval steps. A support bot grounded in your own docs.

## Stacks by budget

| Budget | What you get | Suggested stack |
|---|---|---|
| $0 | Surprisingly much | Free tiers of two assistants, a notebook tool, local models, self-hosted automation, local notes |
| About $20 a month | The biggest jump in capability | One paid assistant with connectors, plus free everything else |
| About $50 a month | A serious daily setup | Paid assistant, a small automation budget, API credits with a limit |
| $100 or more a month | Power-user and builder territory | Higher-tier plans for heavy coding, a creative tool or two, bigger automation plans |

Prices change often. Check the current price before you buy. The shape of the advice does not change: $0 goes far, and about $20 a month is the biggest single upgrade.

## Avoiding tool sprawl

Tool sprawl is the slow creep of subscriptions. Fight it with four habits.

First, audit every three months. List every AI subscription. Cancel anything unused for 30 days. Second, prefer platforms over point tools. One assistant with connectors beats five single-purpose apps. Third, learn deeply before switching. A new tool feels exciting for a week. Mastery pays for years. Fourth, watch for overlap. Your assistant may already include image generation, research modes, or coding agents. Check before you pay twice.

Evaluate any new tool in five minutes. What job does it do? Does my stack already do that job? What does it cost per month? Can I try it free for a week? If it does a job nothing else does, try it. Otherwise, skip it.

> NOTE: A stack is a living thing, not a one-time purchase. Review it when your work changes, when prices change, and every three months at least.

- **My knowledge hub:** ___

Circle your weakest layer. Then read the part of this book that covers it. No automation? Look at the automation chapters. No connections? Look at the connector chapters. Future you will thank present you.

## When to change your stack

A good stack is not forever. Change it when your life changes. A new job may mean a new email system. A new hobby may mean a new creative tool. A tight month may mean dropping a paid plan for a while. Change it when a tool stops earning its keep. If you have not opened it in 30 days, cancel it. You can always resubscribe. Change it when a better option appears, but only after a fair test. Run your ten-task eval from chapter 2.37 on the new tool and the old one. Switch only if the new one wins clearly. And change it when prices move. A price rise is a good moment to audit. Ask: would I buy this today, at this price, knowing what I know now? If the answer is no, cancel it. Your stack should follow your life, not the other way round.

> NOTE: The best stack is the one you actually use. A perfect stack on paper that you never open is worth nothing.

**Answer:** Main assistant, connections, automation, knowledge hub, and builder tools.

**Answer:** For automations, your own apps, scripts, and bots. Set a monthly spend limit first.

**Answer:** One paid assistant plan, at about $20 a month, with connectors turned on.

**Answer:** Every three months, and cancel anything unused for 30 days.

**Answer:** Keep one paid main assistant and use a second one's free tier for second opinions on important questions.

## In short

- Five layers: assistant, connections, automation, knowledge hub, builder tools.
- Choose your main assistant by where you already live and what you do most.
- Subscriptions for daily use. The API, with spend limits, for automations and apps.
- $0 goes far, and about $20 a month is the biggest single upgrade.
- Audit every three months to avoid tool sprawl.
- Fill in the worksheet, then fix your weakest layer first.
