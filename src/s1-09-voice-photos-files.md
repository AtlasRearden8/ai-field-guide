# 1.9 Talk, show, and snap: voice, photos, and files
<!-- original: ch. 09 -->

You do not have to type. That is the good news in this chapter. You can talk to your assistant. You can show it a photo. You can hand it a file. Each way in is good for different jobs. This chapter shows you which to use, and when.

## Talk to it: voice mode

Most assistants have a microphone button. Tap it. Speak. The assistant listens, answers, and often speaks back.

Voice is best when your hands are busy, when you are walking, or when typing feels slow. It is also the most natural way to learn. Talking to a patient tutor beats reading a manual.

Try it like this. Tap the microphone. Say: "Explain what a mortgage is, in plain English, in under a minute." Listen to the answer. Then say: "Give me an example with real numbers." It feels like a phone call with a very patient expert.

**Voice tips:**

- Speak normally. You do not need to shout or speak like a robot.
- Say "stop" or tap the screen to interrupt it. You are in charge.
- Ask it to slow down: "Speak more slowly." Or to keep it short: "Give me the short version."
- Voice chats can be saved as text in most apps, so you can read them later.

> NOTE: Voice mode needs a quiet place and a decent connection. In a noisy street, typing wins. In your kitchen, voice wins.

## Show it a photo: the camera

Your phone's camera is an input device for AI. Tap the camera or image button in the app. Take a photo, or pick one from your gallery. Then ask about it.

Photos are good for jobs where words are hard. "What is this plant?" "What does this warning light mean?" "What is wrong with this bike tyre?" "Is this mushroom safe to eat?" A photo says what a paragraph cannot.

**Photo tips:**

- Get close. Fill the frame with the thing you care about.
- Take it in good light. A dark, blurry photo gives a dark, blurry answer.
- Ask one clear question about the photo. "What is this?" works. "Tell me everything about this" does not.
- For plants, animals, and food, ask the AI how sure it is. Then check with a second source before you eat, touch, or treat anything.

> TRY IT: Open your fridge. Take a photo. Ask: "What can I make for dinner tonight with what you can see here? Give me two ideas." This is the fastest way to feel what photo input can do.

## Hand it a file: documents and PDFs

Most assistants let you upload a file: a PDF, a photo of a letter, a spreadsheet, a document. Tap the plus or paperclip button. Pick the file. Then ask about it.

Files are good for long or confusing documents. A bill. An insurance letter. A school policy. A lease. A manual for an appliance. Upload it, then ask: "Explain this in plain English. What is it about, what do I need to do, and by when?"

Then ask precise questions: "Answer only from this document: what is the fee if I cancel early?" The words "only from this document" matter. They tell the AI to stick to the file, not to guess from general knowledge.

**File tips:**

- Remove private numbers first, if you can: account numbers, ID numbers, passwords.
- One document at a time works best. Two or three is fine. Ten is a mess.
- Ask for a summary first, then ask details. It is faster than reading the whole file yourself.
- Always check the important numbers against the original. AI can misread a figure.

## Make pictures: image generation

Many assistants can also make images. You describe what you want, and it draws it. This is called **image generation**.

Try: "Draw a cosy watercolour picture of a cat asleep on a pile of books, by a window, in the evening."

The first picture may not be perfect. Steer it like a chat: "Make the cat orange." "Add a cup of tea." "Make it brighter."

Image generation is covered in depth in Chapter 4.22. For now, know that it exists, that it is fun, and that it is best for illustrations, cards, and ideas, not for photos of real people or real places.

| Input | Best for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Voice | Hands-free chats, learning, brainstorming | Noise, long answers you cannot skim |
| Photos | Identifying things, fridge meals, warning lights | Blurry photos, safety checks need a second source |
| Files | Long letters, bills, manuals, summaries | Private numbers, misread figures |
| Image making | Cards, illustrations, ideas, fun | Not real photos, hands and text can look odd |

## Mixing inputs together

The real power comes when you mix the inputs. Voice plus a photo. A file plus a question. A photo plus voice. The assistant can handle more than one kind of input at a time.

Here are three combinations worth trying.

**Photo plus voice.** Take a photo of a broken appliance. Then speak: "What is this part, and can I fix it myself?" You do not need to type a long description. The photo does the describing. Your voice asks the question.

**File plus voice.** Upload a letter. Then ask out loud: "Explain this to me like I am ten years old." Listening to a plain explanation is often easier than reading one, especially for a long document.

**Photo plus text.** Snap a picture of a handwritten recipe, a whiteboard, or a child's homework. Ask the AI to turn it into typed text, or to explain it. This turns paper into digital work in seconds.

One tip for mixing: give the inputs in order. Photo first, then the question. File first, then the question. The assistant reads them in order, and it works best when the material comes before the ask.

> TRY IT: Take a photo of something with writing on it: a sign, a label, a menu, a handwritten note. Upload it and ask: "Read this out to me, word for word." Then ask: "Now explain what it means." You just used two inputs and two skills in one minute.

## What each assistant offers

The details change fast, and they differ by plan. As a rough guide, as of late 2026: ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Copilot, Grok, and Meta AI all offer voice chat, photo input, and file upload on their free or paid plans. Image making is built into ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot, Grok, and Meta AI, and available in Claude through connected tools. Check your assistant's own help pages for what is included in your plan, because features move between free and paid.

> NOTE: Features change. A button that exists today may move tomorrow. If you cannot find voice, camera, or file upload, look in your assistant's help pages, or ask the assistant itself: "How do I upload a photo in this app?"

## A small warning about photos of people

Photos of people deserve care. Do not upload photos of other people without their permission. Do not upload photos of children to a chatbot, ever, unless you have thought hard about it. And remember that a photo you upload is data you have shared. Chapter 1.11 covers sharing rules in full.

## Check yourself

1. When is voice mode better than typing?
**Answer:** When your hands are busy, when you are walking, or when you want a natural back-and-forth, like a lesson.

2. What makes a good photo for the AI to read?
**Answer:** Close up, well lit, with the thing you care about filling the frame, plus one clear question.

3. Why should you say "only from this document" when asking about a file?
**Answer:** It tells the AI to stick to the file instead of guessing from general knowledge.

4. What should you remove from a document before you upload it?
**Answer:** Private numbers, like account numbers, ID numbers, and passwords.

5. What is image generation, and what is it best for?
**Answer:** The AI draws a picture from your description. It is best for illustrations, cards, and ideas, not for real photos.

## In short

- You can talk, show a photo, or hand over a file. Typing is only one way in.
- Voice is best hands-free. Photos are best when words are hard. Files are best for long documents.
- Ask one clear question per photo or file.
- Say "only from this document" to keep answers grounded.
- Remove private numbers before you upload anything.
- Image making is fun and useful for illustrations, not for real photos.
