# 3.1 Notion AI: build a second brain
<!-- original: ch. 54 -->

Your notes are only useful if you can find them again. Most of us have the opposite problem. We save links, ideas, and meeting notes in ten places. Then we forget they exist. Notion AI turns that pile into a second brain. It is a place where your notes answer back.

Notion is a notes app. You write pages in it. You can also make tables, lists, and simple databases. A database in Notion is just a table with superpowers. Each row is a page. Each column is a property, like a date or a tag. Notion AI is the built-in helper that reads, writes, and searches all of it.

A second brain is a trusted place outside your head. You put facts there so your mind stays free. The goal is simple. Capture fast. Find fast. Trust what you find.

## Start with one small system

Do not build a giant system on day one. Giant systems die. Start with three databases. That is enough for almost anyone.

First, a notes database. Every note gets a title, a date, and a few tags. Tags are labels like "work", "ideas", or "recipes". Second, a tasks database. Each task has a status, like "to do", "doing", or "done". Third, a reading list. Each item has a source link and a one-line summary.

Keep the columns few. Five columns is plenty. You can add more later. A simple system you actually use beats a perfect system you avoid.

Notion AI helps in two ways. It can fill in blanks for you. It can also answer questions about your notes. This is called autofill. You ask it to write a summary for each row. Or to pick tags for each note. Or to pull out the action items from a meeting page.

> NOTE: Notion AI is a paid add-on in some plans, and included in others. Check the current price on Notion's site before you buy. The free plan still lets you build the databases in this chapter.

Start with one page. Call it "My second brain". Link your three databases on that page. That page becomes your home base. Open it each morning. Close it each night.

## The toolbox: what Notion AI can do

Notion AI has a few core tricks. Learn them one at a time.

Ask it to summarise. Paste in long meeting notes. Ask for the three key points and the action items. It will pull them out in seconds. Ask it to draft. Give it a rough idea. Ask for a first draft of an email, a post, or a plan. You edit the draft. The draft is never the final word. You are.

Ask it to translate. It can turn a page into another language. Ask it to fix tone. "Make this friendlier" or "make this shorter" both work. Ask it to brainstorm. Stuck on names for a project? Ask for twenty ideas. Pick the best three. Throw the rest away.

The newest trick is the agent. Notion Agent is an AI helper that can work across many pages at once. You can ask it a question like "What did we decide about the launch date?" It searches your workspace and answers with links to the source pages. Always click the links. Check that the answer matches the source.

You can also build a custom agent. A custom agent is an agent with a narrow job and clear rules. For example, "Every Friday, read my tasks database and draft my weekly review." You write the instructions once. The agent follows them each time.

> TRY IT: Open Notion and make a page called "Test note". Write five messy sentences about your day. Select the text. Ask Notion AI to summarise it in one sentence and list any tasks it can see. Notice what it gets right and what it invents.

## Capture from everywhere, then review weekly

A second brain only works if things go into it. Make capture easy. Use the Notion app on your phone. Use the web clipper to save articles. Use the quick capture button. If capture takes more than ten seconds, you will stop doing it.

Meeting notes deserve a small workflow. During a meeting, write rough notes on one page. After the meeting, ask Notion AI to clean them up. Ask for decisions, action items, and owners. An owner is the person who will do the task. Move each action item into your tasks database. Give it a date.

Once a week, do a review. This takes twenty minutes. Ask your agent or Notion AI to look at your tasks and notes from the week. Ask three questions. What did I finish? What is stuck? What should I drop? Move stuck items to a new date or delete them. A brain full of dead tasks is not a second brain. It is a junk drawer.

Linking matters too. Link related pages together. A note about a book links to the project it inspired. A meeting page links to the task it created. Links turn a pile of pages into a web. AI search works better when pages link to each other.

> NOTE: Never put passwords, bank details, or medical records in Notion. Notion is a notes app, not a vault. Keep secrets in a password manager instead.

## Power moves and gotchas

Here are twelve small moves that add up. Use autofill to tag new notes. Use AI to write a one-line summary for every article you save. Ask AI to turn a messy brainstorm into a clean list. Build a template for meeting notes with an AI summary block built in. Make a dashboard page that shows today's tasks and this week's notes. Use the search box as a question box. Ask "What did I write about gardening?" instead of guessing keywords.

Connect Notion to other tools if you want. Notion works with automation tools like n8n and Zapier. That is covered in Section 4. For now, know that it is possible. A simple start is a form that drops answers into a Notion database.

Watch the gotchas. AI can invent facts. It can quote a page that says no such thing. Always check answers against the source page. Permissions matter in shared workspaces. If you share a page, the AI can read it too. Do not share pages with private notes. Credits and limits change. Some plans cap how much AI you can use. Check your plan before a big project.

Meeting notes deserve their own workflow. Before a meeting, make a page with the agenda and the people attending. During the meeting, write rough notes. Do not worry about spelling. After the meeting, ask Notion AI to clean the notes up. Ask for three things. Decisions made. Tasks with owners. Questions left open. Move each task into your tasks database. Link the meeting page to the project page. The whole job takes five minutes. A meeting with no written outcome is a meeting you will repeat.

Notion also connects in both directions with other tools. MCP is a standard plug that lets AI use your tools. It is covered in Section 2. With MCP, an assistant like Claude can read your Notion pages and answer questions about them. It can also write new pages for you. That is one direction. The other direction is Notion calling out. An automation can send a Notion change to another app. A new task in Notion can create a calendar event. A finished project can post a message to your team chat. Start with one connection. Test it for a week. Add a second only when the first one works.

## Check yourself

1. What is a second brain, in your own words?
**Answer:** A trusted place outside your head where you store notes and facts, so you can find them again and free up your mind.

2. What three databases should you start with?
**Answer:** A notes database, a tasks database, and a reading list.

3. What is autofill in Notion AI?
**Answer:** AI filling in properties for you, such as writing a summary, picking tags, or pulling out action items for each row.

4. What is a Notion Agent?
**Answer:** An AI helper that can search across many pages at once and answer questions with links to the source pages.

5. Why should you do a weekly review?
**Answer:** To finish, unstick, or drop tasks, so your system stays current instead of turning into a junk drawer of dead tasks.

## In short

- A second brain is a trusted place for notes you can search and trust.
- Start small: notes, tasks, and a reading list, with few columns.
- Notion AI can summarise, draft, translate, fix tone, and brainstorm.
- Agents can answer questions across your whole workspace, with links.
- Capture fast, link pages together, and review for twenty minutes weekly.
- Always check AI answers against the source page, and never store secrets in Notion.
