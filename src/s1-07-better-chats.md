# 1.7 Better chats: follow-ups and fixing bad answers
<!-- original: ch. 07 -->

Your first prompt is rarely your best prompt. That is fine. A chat is a conversation, not a search box. The real skill is not writing one perfect message. It is steering the conversation until the answer is right.

This chapter teaches you how to follow up, correct the AI, and rescue a chat that has gone wrong.

## The follow-up is the superpower

Beginners type one message, read the answer, and stop. Experienced users keep going. They treat the first answer as a draft, not a final result.

Here are the follow-up phrases that do the most work. Learn them by heart.

- "Make it shorter."
- "Make it warmer."
- "Give me an example."
- "Explain that like I am ten years old."
- "Now do it as a table."
- "Add one more idea, but make it cheaper."
- "What would you change, and why?"

Each one is a small steering wheel turn. Three or four turns, and the answer is yours.

> TRY IT: Take the last answer your assistant gave you. Reply with just two words: "Make it shorter." Then reply: "Now make it friendlier." Watch how fast it adapts. That is the follow-up in action.

## Steer with verbs, not hints

Vague steering gets vague results. "Make it better" means nothing. The AI does not know what "better" means to you.

Steer with clear verbs. Tell it exactly what to change.

- Shorter, longer, simpler, warmer, firmer, funnier.
- Add an example. Remove the jargon. Use bullets.
- Start with the most important point.
- Write it for a busy manager. Write it for a ten-year-old.

One clear instruction beats five vague ones.

## Correct it like a colleague

AI gets things wrong. When it does, correct it directly. Do not be polite about the error. Be clear.

Say: "That is not right. The meeting is on Tuesday, not Monday. Please fix the plan."

Then check the new answer. The AI will usually correct itself and carry on. It does not get offended. It does not remember the mistake as a grudge. It just adjusts.

If it argues, or repeats the error, do not fight it. Check the fact yourself. Then paste in the correct fact and say: "Use this fact from now on in this chat."

> NOTE: Correcting the AI teaches it about this chat only. It does not fix the AI for everyone. Your correction stays in your conversation.

## Ask it to check itself

You can ask the AI to be its own critic. This is one of the most useful tricks in the book.

- "Read your answer again. What is weak or missing?"
- "What would a sceptic say about this plan?"
- "Be brutally honest: is this a good idea?"
- "List three things that could go wrong with this."

The AI will often spot its own gaps. It is not perfect at this. But it is much better than staring at a smooth answer and assuming it is right.

## Give it a role

A simple trick that changes everything: tell the AI who to be.

"Act as a patient teacher." "Act as a tough job interviewer." "Act as a friendly travel guide who hates tourist traps."

A role changes the tone, the detail, and the kind of answer you get. A teacher explains and quizzes. An interviewer asks hard questions. A travel guide skips the obvious sights.

Roles work best when you add a task: "Act as a personal trainer. Make me a 4-week plan for someone who has never exercised."

## When to start a new chat

Sometimes steering stops working. The chat gets muddled. The AI keeps repeating an old mistake. Or it mixes two topics together.

That is the signal to start fresh. Click "New chat". Then paste in a short summary of what you need, with the key facts. For example:

"Help me plan a birthday party for my daughter, aged 8, on June 14. Ten children. Garden party. Budget £100. No nuts, one child is allergic."

A clean start, with the facts up front, beats ten rounds of fixing a tangled chat.

## When the AI keeps missing the point

Sometimes steering does not work. You say "shorter" and it gets longer. You say "simpler" and it adds jargon. The AI has misunderstood your goal, not your words.

When that happens, try one of these three moves.

**Restate the goal, not the fix.** Instead of "make it better", say what the answer is for. "This is for a text to my mum, who is 80 and does not like long messages." The goal tells the AI what "better" means.

**Break the job into pieces.** Ask for step one only. "First, give me just the three main points. Nothing else." Get those right. Then say: "Now turn point one into a paragraph." Small steps are easier to steer than big ones.

**Give it a constraint it cannot miss.** "Use only words a ten-year-old would know." "No sentence longer than 12 words." "Start every bullet with a verb." A hard rule forces a different answer.

If all three fail, the problem may be the chat itself. The AI may be stuck on an old instruction from earlier in the conversation. Start a new chat. Paste in your goal and your facts. Begin clean. This fixes the problem more often than any clever prompt.

> NOTE: Frustration is information. If you are annoyed with the AI, stop and ask: what did I actually want? Write that down in one sentence. Then paste that sentence as your next prompt. It works surprisingly often.

## A real example, start to finish

Watch how a chat improves, turn by turn.

**You:** "Write an email asking for a pay rise."

**AI:** Gives a long, stiff, formal letter.

**You:** "Shorter. Warmer. I have worked here for three years and I love the team."

**AI:** Gives a shorter, warmer draft that mentions your three years.

**You:** "Add one sentence about the new project I led last month."

**AI:** Adds it, smoothly.

**You:** "Read it again. What would my manager think?"

**AI:** Points out that the tone is good but the ask is buried. Suggests moving it earlier.

**You:** "Do that."

Three minutes. Five turns. A good email. That is what better chats look like.

## Check yourself

1. Why is the follow-up more important than the first prompt?
**Answer:** Because the first answer is a draft. Follow-ups steer it until it is right, and you do not need a perfect first message.

2. What is wrong with saying "Make it better"?
**Answer:** "Better" is vague. The AI does not know what better means to you. Use clear verbs like shorter, warmer, or simpler.

3. How should you correct the AI when it is wrong?
**Answer:** Say clearly what is wrong and what the right fact is, then ask it to try again.

4. What does asking the AI to "be brutally honest" do?
**Answer:** It asks the AI to criticise its own answer, which often reveals gaps or weak points.

5. When should you start a new chat instead of steering?
**Answer:** When the chat is muddled, the AI keeps repeating an old mistake, or two topics have got mixed together.

## In short

- The first answer is a draft. Steer it with follow-ups.
- Use clear verbs: shorter, warmer, simpler, firmer.
- Correct errors directly, with the right fact.
- Ask the AI to check its own work.
- Give it a role: teacher, interviewer, guide.
- When a chat tangles, start fresh with the facts up front.
