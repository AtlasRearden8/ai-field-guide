# 4.21 The multimodal playground
<!-- original: ch. 83 -->

Text is only the start. Today's AI can also see, hear, and make things.

It can look at a photo and tell you what is in it. It can listen to a voice note and write it out. It can make a picture, a short video, a song, or a spoken story.

That is what **multimodal** means. It is a fancy word for a simple idea. The AI works with more than one kind of input. It works with text, images, sound, and video.

This chapter is a tour. You will taste each kind of creative AI. Then you will chain them together into one small project.

## The map: what each kind of AI does

Start with the big picture. There are four kinds of input. There are four kinds of output.

**Images in.** Show the AI a photo, a screenshot, or a drawing. Ask what it shows. Ask it to read the handwriting. Ask it to spot what is wrong in a picture.

**Sound in.** Play the AI a voice note, a song, or a meeting recording. Ask for a written summary. Ask what was decided. Ask for the key quotes.

**Video in.** Share a clip or a link to a video. Ask what happens in it. Ask for the main points. Some assistants can watch long videos and answer questions about them.

**Screens in.** Take a screenshot of your computer or phone. Ask what you are looking at. Ask what to click next. This one habit saves hours.

Then there are the outputs. **Images out** means pictures from a written description. **Video out** means short clips from a description or a still image. **Voice out** means spoken words in a natural voice. **Music out** means songs and tunes from a prompt.

> NOTE: Tools change fast. New versions appear every month. Some tools vanish. Learn the kinds of tools, not just the names. Your skills will carry over when the names change.

## Show, do not just type

Most people only type to AI. Power users show things too.

A screenshot beats a long description. Your screen shows an error message. Do not type it out word by word. Take a screenshot and ask what it means.

A photo beats a guess. Your plant has spots on its leaves. Take a photo. Ask what might be wrong and what to do next.

A voice note beats typing on the move. Record your idea while you walk. Ask the AI to turn it into a neat list.

Try this with each major assistant. Claude, ChatGPT, and Gemini all accept images and files. Many also handle audio and video. Gemini is especially strong with long videos and video links.

Here is a simple test. Take a photo of your fridge. Ask what meals you could make from what you see. You will learn more from that one test than from a whole page of theory.

## Chaining: where the magic happens

One tool alone is fun. Several tools together are magic. **Chaining** means the output of one tool becomes the input of the next tool.

Here is a chain that makes a whole storybook in about an hour.

First, ask Claude to write a short story. Ask it for three picture prompts too. Ask it for a narration script. Ask it for song lyrics. Keep them all in the same style.

Next, paste the picture prompts into an image tool. You get three illustrations that match the story.

Then paste the narration script into a voice tool such as ElevenLabs. You get a warm spoken version of the story.

Then paste the lyrics into a music tool such as Suno. You get a theme song.

Finally, put it all together in a simple video editor such as CapCut or Descript. Pictures, voice, and music become one little film.

You are the director. The AI tools are your crew. Claude can be your creative director. It keeps the story, the pictures, the voice, and the song in step with each other.

## Make things with care

Creative AI is powerful. Use it with care.

Ask before you use a real person's photo or voice. Do not upload photos of children to public tools. Do not copy a living artist's style and sell it as your own work.

Label AI-made work where people could be misled. If a picture looks like a real photo but is not, say so. If a voice is not a real person, say so.

Check the rules of each tool before you sell anything. Some free plans allow personal use only. Some allow commercial use. The terms differ. Read them first.

## A closer look at each medium

**Pictures.** Image tools turn words into pictures. They can also edit a picture you already have. Change the sky. Remove a person from the background. Turn a photo into a watercolour. You will learn the full craft in the next chapter.

**Video.** Video tools turn words into short moving clips. They can also animate a still picture. A photo of a lake can gain gentle ripples. A drawing can gain a slow camera move. You will learn the full craft in chapter 4.23.

**Voice.** Voice tools turn text into speech. They can read a story aloud. They can translate your words into another language. They can also clone a voice, but only with clear permission. You will learn more in chapter 4.25.

**Music.** Music tools turn words into songs. Give them a style and a topic. They return a full song with singing. You will learn more in chapter 4.24.

**Words.** The chat assistant ties it all together. It writes the story. It writes the prompts. It writes the script. It is the one tool you use at every step.

Each medium has its own strengths. Pictures are fast to make. Video takes longer. Voice is great for listening on the move. Music sets a mood in seconds. Pick the medium that fits the job.

## Twenty creative experiments to try

You do not need a big project to learn. Small experiments teach fast. Try one a day.

- Make a portrait of your pet as a Renaissance painting.
- Turn a family photo into a storybook illustration.
- Design a poster for a party that has not happened yet.
- Make a logo for an imaginary shop.
- Create a wallpaper for your phone in your favourite colours.
- Dub a short video of yourself into Spanish.
- Make a theme song for your morning run.
- Turn your notes into a two-voice podcast.
- Animate an old photo with a gentle breeze.
- Make a comic strip about your day.
- Design a book cover for a story you wrote.
- Create a birthday card with a singing message.
- Turn a recipe into a short video with narration.
- Make a map of an imaginary island.
- Create a soundtrack for a rainy afternoon.
- Turn a child's drawing into a polished picture.
- Make a trailer for a film that does not exist.
- Create a set of icons for your phone apps.
- Turn a poem into a short animated film.
- Make a jingle for your favourite snack.

Each experiment takes minutes. Each one teaches you a new skill. Keep the ones you like. Throw the rest away.

Share your best experiments with a friend. Show them the picture, play them the song, or read them the story. A small thing you made is worth more than a big thing you only planned. Making is the fastest way to learn what these tools can really do, and what they cannot do yet.

> TRY IT: Pick a theme you love. It could be your pet, your street, or a childhood memory. Ask Claude to write a 200-word story about it, plus two image prompts and a short narration script. Make the two pictures. Listen to the story read aloud by a voice tool. You have just made a tiny multimedia storybook.

## Check yourself

1. Your laptop shows a confusing settings screen. What is the fastest way to get help?
**Answer:** Take a screenshot and ask the AI what you are looking at. It sees exactly what you see.

2. What does the word multimodal mean in plain words?
**Answer:** It means the AI can work with more than one kind of input and output, such as text, images, sound, and video.

3. What is chaining?
**Answer:** It is linking tools together so one tool's output becomes the next tool's input.

4. Why should you label AI-made pictures that look like real photos?
**Answer:** So people are not misled into thinking something fake is real.

## In short

- Multimodal means AI that works with text, images, sound, and video.
- Showing the AI a photo or screenshot is often faster than describing it.
- Each tool does one job well, and chaining tools together makes bigger things.
- Claude can act as your creative director and keep a project consistent.
- Tools change fast, so learn the kinds of tools and the skills behind them.
- Make things with care: ask permission, label AI work, and check the rules.
