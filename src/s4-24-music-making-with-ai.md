# 4.24 Music making with AI
<!-- original: ch. 86 -->

AI can now write and sing a full song from one sentence. That is only the start.

It can also help you write lyrics. It can teach you music theory. It can split a song into its separate instruments so you can practise along. It can polish your recordings. It can even control a real music app while you watch.

This chapter covers the song makers, the tools for real musicians, a songwriting workflow, and the rights you need to understand before you share or sell a song.

## The music landscape

**Full songs with vocals.** Suno is the best-known tool here. Give it a style and a topic. It returns a complete song with singing. You can also give it your own lyrics.

**Songwriting partners.** Claude, ChatGPT, and Gemini help with lyrics, chords, and theory. They do not sing. They help you think.

**Instrumentals and sound design.** ElevenLabs Music and Stable Audio make tunes without singing. They are good for jingles, background music, and sound effects.

**Practice tools.** Moises, LALAL.AI, and Demucs split a song into parts. Take out the guitar. Play along as the guitarist. Slow a tricky solo down without changing its pitch.

**Production apps.** GarageBand, Ableton, and BandLab are **DAWs**. A DAW is a digital audio workstation, which is a program for recording and mixing music. AI features now live inside them. Session players can add a drummer or a bassist that follows your song.

> NOTE: The big 2026 shift is licensed music. After lawsuits from record labels, AI music moved to licensed training. Suno's v6 models, released in September 2026, were trained on licensed music from label partners. Paid plans cap downloads at 20 a month on Pro. Udio took a different path. Its downloads are switched off, and its terms now allow personal, non-commercial use only. Use Udio to try ideas, not to ship songs. Terms differ by plan, so read them before you publish anything. Check the current price on the tool's own site.

## Make your first AI song

Making a song takes five steps.

1. **Pick a style.** Name the genre, the mood, the instruments, the tempo, and the voice. Try this: "Upbeat indie pop, jangly guitars, handclaps, female vocals, summery, 120 BPM." BPM means beats per minute. It sets how fast the song feels.
2. **Write or generate lyrics.** Your own words give the best results. They make the song personal. They also strengthen your claim to the song.
3. **Use structure tags.** Tags tell the generator the shape of the song. Use `[Intro]`, `[Verse 1]`, `[Pre-Chorus]`, `[Chorus]`, `[Bridge]`, and `[Outro]`. Your lyrics land in the right sections.
4. **Generate several versions.** Pick the best one. The first version is rarely the best.
5. **Iterate.** Extend the song. Replace one section. Change the style. Remix it.

Here is a tiny example of lyrics with tags:

> [Verse 1] Biscuit's at the window, tail going like a drum. Every car is a mailman, every bird is a chum.
> [Chorus] Oh Biscuit, you're the sunshine on my street!

Ask Claude to write lyrics for you. Give it details. "Write lyrics for a birthday song for my friend Sam. She loves hiking, is terrible at karaoke, is always late, and has a heart of gold. Upbeat country, verse, chorus, and bridge structure, clean enough for her grandma. Include one inside joke about the 2024 camping trip tent disaster." Then paste the lyrics into your song generator.

## A songwriting workflow with AI

AI works best as a partner, not a replacement. You bring the idea and the taste. AI brings speed and options.

**Brainstorm.** Ask for ten fresh angles on a theme. "Ten fresh angles for a song about moving to a new city." Pick the one that excites you.

**Lyrics.** Ask for rhymes, images, and word counts. "Give me five alternatives for line three that rhyme with rain and keep eight syllables."

**Chords.** Ask for progressions that fit a mood. "A bittersweet chord progression in G, with a surprising chord in the chorus."

**Melody.** Ask for a rhythm or a set of notes. Or generate a demo and learn to play it yourself.

**Demo.** Generate a quick demo with your lyrics. Listen. Keep what works. Change what does not.

**Feedback.** Ask for a kind critique. "Critique these lyrics like a supportive songwriting teacher." Fix the weak lines. Keep your voice.

Always ask for options, not answers. Ten options let you choose. One answer makes you a passenger. The best idea is often a mix of two options.

## AI for musicians who play

If you already play an instrument, AI gives you superpowers.

**Remove any instrument.** Use Moises, LALAL.AI, or Demucs. Take the guitar out of a song. Play along as the guitarist. Take the bass out. Practise your bass lines.

**Slow down without pitch change.** Learn a tricky solo at sixty percent speed. The notes stay in tune. Your fingers catch up.

**Chord detection.** Use Moises or Chordify. See the chords of any song as it plays. Learn songs by ear, faster.

**Theory tutor.** Ask Claude or ChatGPT. "Why does the IV-to-iv change sound so sad? Show me in C major." You get an answer in plain words, with examples.

**Session players.** Logic Pro and BandLab offer AI drummers and bassists. They follow your song. They keep time while you play.

**Mastering.** LANDR and DAW assistants polish your recording. **Mastering** is the final polish that makes a home recording sound ready to release. It balances volume and tone.

**Mixing feedback.** Describe your mix to Claude. Or share a screenshot of your levels. "My vocals sound buried. What should I try first?" You get practical steps, not jargon.

## Claude and your DAW, through MCP

**MCP** is a standard plug that lets AI use your tools. Community MCP servers connect Claude to music apps like Ableton Live. Then you can say things like:

> "Create a four-bar lo-fi drum loop at 85 BPM on a new track."
> "Add a warm Rhodes piano playing Am7, Dmaj7, Gmaj7, Cmaj7."
> "Put reverb on the vocals and turn the drums down 3 dB."

Claude does it in your real project. It is early and delightfully nerdy. It is also a great example of MCP turning an assistant into a studio intern.

Keep a backup of your project first. AI can change settings fast. A backup lets you undo anything.

## Music for your projects

Songs need music. Videos need background tunes. Podcasts need jingles. Here is how to choose.

Use AI-made instrumentals for background music. They are quick and cheap. Check the licence first. Some plans allow commercial use. Some do not.

Use your own songs for personal projects. A birthday song for a friend is a gift. A lullaby with your child's name in it is a keepsake.

Do not use copyrighted songs as input unless you have the rights. Remixing other people's music follows the platform's licence. Do not clone a real artist's voice. Do not copy a famous song and call it yours.

The law is still moving. AI music rights are being shaped by lawsuits and licensing deals right now. For anything commercial, check the current terms. For a serious release, get proper advice.

## Check yourself

1. What are structure tags like `[Verse]` and `[Chorus]` for?
**Answer:** They tell the song generator the shape of the song, so your lyrics land in the right sections.

2. You want to practise bass along to a song. Which AI tool helps most?
**Answer:** A stem separation tool such as Moises, LALAL.AI, or Demucs, to remove the bass, plus slow-down to learn the tricky parts.

3. Can you always sell songs you made on a free plan?
**Answer:** Not necessarily. Commercial use depends on the tool's terms and your plan. Check before selling or uploading to streaming.

4. What changed with Suno in September 2026?
**Answer:** Its v6 models were trained on licensed music from label partners, and paid plans cap downloads at 20 a month on Pro.

5. What is the rule for Udio now?
**Answer:** Downloads are switched off, and its terms allow personal, non-commercial use only.

## In short

- Suno makes full songs; its v6 models use licensed music, with download caps on paid plans.
- Udio is for trying ideas only: no downloads, personal use only.
- Your own lyrics make songs personal and strengthen your authorship.
- Structure tags give a song its shape: intro, verse, chorus, bridge, outro.
- Musicians get practice superpowers: stem separation, slow-down, chord detection, and a theory tutor.
- MCP lets Claude operate real music software such as Ableton.
- Check the terms before you publish or sell anything.
