# 2.7 Perplexity, start to finish
<!-- original: ch. 23 -->

Perplexity calls itself an "answer engine." Ask a question. You get a clear, written answer with numbered sources you can click.

It is like a search engine and a research assistant rolled into one. It is the go-to tool for fact-finding, comparisons, product research, and staying on top of news.

If you care about where an answer came from, this is your tool.

## Getting started

You need nothing to start. An account saves your history.

1. Go to **perplexity.ai** or install the app. Check the maker is Perplexity AI.
2. Ask a question right away. You can use it without an account.
3. Sign up with email, Google, or Apple to save your **Library** of past searches.
4. Personalize it. Go to Settings, then **Personalization**. Tell it about you: your location, your interests, how you like answers.
5. Try the **Discover** tab for AI-summarized news tailored to your interests.

A **citation** is a numbered source link, like [1] or [2]. Click it to open the page the answer came from. Good citations are the whole point of Perplexity.

> NOTE: Perplexity is less of a chatty friend than ChatGPT or Claude. That is by design. It is built for facts first, chat second. If you want long creative writing or personal chit-chat, another assistant may suit you better.

## What it is best at

**Search modes.** Perplexity has four gears. Pick the right one for the job.

| Mode | Use it for |
|---|---|
| Quick search | A fast fact or a simple question |
| Pro Search | A harder question, with more sources and deeper reasoning |
| Research | A long, structured report with many sources |
| Labs | A finished output, like a dashboard, a spreadsheet, or a small web page |

You can also steer where it searches. Pick the web, **academic papers**, **social discussions**, **finance data**, or your own files. That steering is called **Focus**.

**Reading sources like a pro.** Every Perplexity answer has numbered citations. Use them.

1. Hover or tap a number to see the source.
2. Open the ones behind important claims. Does the page actually say that?
3. Check the source quality. An official site or a major publication beats a random blog or a forum.
4. Check the dates. Is the information current?
5. Ask follow-ups. Try: "Which of these sources are most reliable?" Or: "Find a primary source for claim 3."

A **primary source** is the original place a fact comes from. A government report, a company's own page, or the study itself. Secondary sources report on it second-hand.

This habit makes Perplexity one of the most trustworthy ways to use AI for facts. But it is not magic. Perplexity can still misread a weak source. Click through for anything important.

**Spaces.** A **Space** is a research folder for one topic, with custom instructions and uploaded files.

1. In the sidebar, pick **Spaces**, then **Create a Space**. Name it "Buying our first home."
2. Add instructions: "We are first-time buyers in Ohio, budget $300,000, two kids. Prioritize official and local sources." Add files, like a pre-approval letter or listings.
3. Every search in the Space uses that context. Share it with your partner to research together.

**Comet: the AI browser.** **Comet** is Perplexity's free web browser, for Windows, Mac, iPhone, and Android. It looks like Chrome and imports your bookmarks. But it has an AI assistant on every page.

Try: "Summarize this article and tell me what is missing from it." Or: "Compare the prices and reviews of this blender across my open tabs." Or: "Find me a table for four at an Italian restaurant near here on Friday at 7." The assistant navigates for you and asks before booking.

**Comet Plus** costs $5 a month and is included with Pro and Max. It adds publisher content. Check the current price.

**Connectors, files, and more.** Link Gmail, Google Calendar, Outlook, Notion, and others. Perplexity can then search your own content alongside the web. Upload PDFs, spreadsheets, and images and ask about them.

**Shopping and finance.** Perplexity does product research with comparisons. In some countries you can buy directly. Its finance pages show stock and company pages with AI summaries of earnings and news. **Voice mode** lets you ask questions out loud in the app.

> TRY IT: Ask Perplexity a question you have been meaning to research: a purchase, a health topic to discuss with your doctor, or a trip. Open three of the sources and check each claim. Then ask the same question in your main assistant and compare. Which answer would you trust more, and why?

## Step-by-step recipes

**Recipe 1: Buy the right thing**

1. Pick Pro Search and ask: "Best cordless vacuum under $300 for a small apartment with a cat. Compare the top 4 in a table: battery life, weight, noise, reviews. Cite recent reviews."
2. Open two review sources before buying. Check the dates on the reviews.
3. Ask a follow-up: "What do owners complain about after six months?"

**Recipe 2: A research report you can trust**

1. Pick Research mode and ask: "What does the evidence say about intermittent fasting for people over 50? Summarize the strongest studies and their limitations."
2. Read the report. Check the citations behind the biggest claims.
3. Take the report, and your questions, to your doctor. Do not act on health advice from AI alone.

**Recipe 3: Plan a move with a Space**

1. Create a Space called "Moving to Denver" with your budget and priorities.
2. Ask about neighborhoods, schools, commute times, and costs. Everything stays in one place.
3. Share the Space with your partner so you research together.

**Recipe 4: Fact-check a viral claim**

1. Ask: "Is it true that this claim is correct? Check fact-checkers and primary sources. Tell me what is confirmed and what is not."
2. Open the fact-checker links yourself. Perplexity can misread a weak source, so your own eyes are the final check.

## Traps to avoid

**A citation is not a guarantee.** Perplexity can misread a source or over-trust a weak one. Always click through for anything important, especially money and health.

**Browser agents need supervision.** An AI that can click around websites while you are logged in is powerful. Web pages can contain hidden instructions trying to trick it. Watch it work. Approve anything important. Keep it away from banking.

**It is not the best writer.** For long creative writing, stories, or warm personal messages, ChatGPT or Claude will usually do better.

**Privacy settings to check.** Go to Settings and look at your data controls. In Comet settings, control what the assistant can see and do, and clear browsing data. Delete old searches from your Library when you are done with them.

**Do not paste secrets.** Do not share passwords, bank details, or medical records. Keep sensitive topics out of any assistant, including this one.

## Check yourself

1. What makes Perplexity especially good for fact-finding?

**Answer:** Every answer comes with numbered citations you can open to verify each claim.

2. You want a long, structured report on home solar panels. Which mode?

**Answer:** Research, the deep research mode. For a dashboard or spreadsheet output, try Labs.

3. What is Comet?

**Answer:** Perplexity's free AI web browser. Its assistant can explain pages, compare tabs, and carry out web tasks with your supervision.

4. What is a Space, and when would you use one?

**Answer:** A research folder for one topic, with custom instructions and files. Use it for a big project like buying a home or planning a move.

5. A Perplexity answer cites a source. Should you trust the claim right away?

**Answer:** Not yet. Open the source. Check that the page actually says it, that the source is reliable, and that the date is current.
