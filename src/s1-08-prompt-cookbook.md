# 1.8 A cookbook of prompts for everyday life
<!-- original: ch. 08 -->

You do not need to invent prompts from scratch. Copy one from this chapter. Swap the words in [brackets] for your own details. Send it. Each prompt is also a small lesson in good prompting: task, context, details, format, tone.

There are 36 prompts here, across everyday jobs. The original book had 101. This chapter gives you the best of them, and the big prompt library at the back of this book has 250 more.

> NOTE: Before you paste in a bill, a letter, or a medical note, remove private numbers: account numbers, ID numbers, and passwords. The AI does not need them, and they should not leave your hands.

## Food and meals

1. "Plan 5 weeknight dinners for [2 adults and a 10-year-old]. We like [pasta, curries, and tacos], dislike [mushrooms], and I have [30 minutes] to cook. Budget-friendly. Give me a shopping list grouped by supermarket section."
2. "What can I make tonight with [chicken, rice, broccoli, and soy sauce]? Give me 3 ideas, each under 30 minutes, with steps."
3. "Turn this recipe into a shopping list for [4 people], and tell me what I can prep the night before: [paste recipe]."
4. "I am vegetarian and I hate washing up. Give me 5 one-pot meals for the week, with cooking times."

## Messages and emails

5. "Write a short, warm text to my neighbour, thanking them for taking in a parcel. Keep it under 40 words."
6. "Draft a polite email to my manager asking to move a meeting from [Tuesday 10am] to [Thursday afternoon]. Give a short reason. Friendly tone."
7. "I need to say no to [an invitation to a work event on Friday]. Write a kind reply that keeps the door open. Under 60 words."
8. "Rewrite this message to sound warmer but still professional: [paste message]."

## Money and paperwork

9. "Explain this letter in plain English. What is it about, what do I need to do, and by when? [paste letter, with private numbers removed]."
10. "I earn [£2,000] a month after tax. Rent is [£800], bills are [£300], food is [£250]. Help me save [£200] a month. Give me a simple weekly plan in a table."
11. "What questions should I ask before I sign this contract? [paste contract or describe it]. List them as bullets, in plain English."
12. "Make a checklist for moving house, from 4 weeks before to moving day. Include bills, address changes, and packing."

## Home and life admin

13. "My [washing machine] is making a loud banging noise when it spins. What are the likely causes, from simplest to check first? What can I try safely, and when should I call a repair person?"
14. "Plan a spring clean for a [3-bedroom house] over one weekend. Break it into 2-hour jobs, room by room."
15. "Write a simple weekly cleaning rota for [2 adults and 2 children], with jobs that take under 20 minutes each."
16. "Help me declutter my [garage]. Give me a keep, donate, sell, and bin plan I can finish in one afternoon."

## Learning something new

17. "Teach me the basics of [how the stock market works] in 5 short lessons. I am a complete beginner. After each lesson, ask me one question, and wait for my answer."
18. "Explain [photosynthesis] like I am ten years old. Use an everyday example. Keep it under 150 words."
19. "Quiz me on [French words for food]. Ask one question at a time. Tell me if I am right, and correct me gently."
20. "Give me a 2-week plan to learn [basic Spanish for a holiday]. 15 minutes a day. Include speaking practice."

## Work

21. "Summarise this meeting note into 5 bullets: decisions made, and who does what by when. [paste notes]."
22. "Turn this rough list into a clear agenda for a 30-minute team meeting: [paste list]."
23. "Write a short, professional out-of-office message for [June 10 to 14]. Say who to contact instead: [name and email]."
24. "Give me 5 ideas for a team social that costs under [£10 per person] and works for people who do not drink alcohol."

## Health and wellbeing

25. "Make me a gentle 4-week walking plan. I currently walk [20 minutes a day]. I want to reach [45 minutes]. Include one rest day a week."
26. "I sleep badly. Ask me 5 questions about my evenings, then suggest 3 small changes I could try this week."
27. "Explain what [blood pressure numbers] mean in plain English. What counts as normal, and when should someone see a doctor? This is for understanding only, not medical advice."

> NOTE: Health prompts are for understanding and preparing, never for replacing a doctor. In an emergency, call your local emergency number. If something worries you, see a real clinician.

## Travel

28. "Plan a relaxed 2-day trip to [Lisbon] for [2 adults] in [May]. We like food markets and sea views. We do not like crowds. Give me a day-by-day plan with times."
29. "What should I pack for [a week in Scotland in October]? Two adults, one child, walking and city days. Give me a checklist."
30. "Translate these 10 useful phrases into [Italian], with a simple pronunciation guide: hello, thank you, where is the toilet, the bill please, and 6 more you think I will need."

## Tech help

35. "My [phone] battery dies by lunchtime. Ask me questions to find out why, then give me 5 fixes, easiest first."

## How to use this cookbook

Pick three prompts today: one useful, one for learning, one just for fun. Customise them. Send them. Star your favourites and save them in your "My prompts" note.

Notice the pattern in every good prompt. Task, context, details, format, tone. The brackets are the details. The rest is the shape. Once you see the shape, you can write your own prompts for any job.

> TRY IT: Write your own prompt right now, for a job you actually have today. Use the five-part shape from Chapter 1.6. Send it. If the answer is good, save the prompt. You just added a recipe to your own cookbook.

## Check yourself

1. What do the [brackets] in a cookbook prompt mean?
**Answer:** They mark the parts you must swap for your own details, like names, dates, and numbers.

2. Why should you remove private numbers before pasting in a letter or bill?
**Answer:** Because the AI does not need them, and private numbers should not be shared.

3. What pattern do all the good prompts in this chapter share?
**Answer:** Task, context, details, format, and tone.

4. Which prompts in this chapter are for understanding only, not for replacing a professional?
**Answer:** The health prompts. They help you prepare and understand, but a doctor is still needed for real advice.

5. What is the best way to build your own cookbook?
**Answer:** Save the prompts that worked well in a note called "My prompts", and add your own as you write them.
