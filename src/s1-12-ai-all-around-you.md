# 1.12 AI all around you
<!-- original: ch. 12 -->

You do not always need to open a chatbot app. AI is already in your phone, your speaker, your car, your glasses, and your browser. This chapter is a tour of the AI you are already using without noticing. Chances are, you own more AI than you realise.

## On iPhone, iPad, and Mac

Apple's AI is called **Apple Intelligence**. It powers a rebuilt, much more capable Siri. It can summarise your messages, rewrite your writing, sort your photos, and answer questions using what is on your device.

The big upgrade arrived with iOS 27 in September 2026. It runs on Apple Foundation Models, built with Google's Gemini technology alongside Apple's own work. It needs an iPhone 15 Pro or later, and it was not available everywhere at launch. Check your device and region if you are unsure.

Handy things to try:

- "Summarise this email thread."
- "Rewrite this message to sound more professional."
- "Find the photo of the dog at the beach."
- Hold the side button and ask Siri a question, the way you always have, only now it understands more.

> NOTE: Apple Intelligence does a lot of its work on your device, not in the cloud. That is good for privacy. It also means older phones cannot run it, because they lack the power.

## On Android phones

On most Android phones, Gemini has replaced the old Google Assistant. Press and hold the home button, or say "Hey Google", and Gemini answers. It can read your screen, help with your Gmail, and plan your day.

Samsung Galaxy phones add **Galaxy AI**: live call translation, summaries of your notes, and photo editing help. Pixel phones add extras like Magic Editor, which can move or remove things in a photo, and Call Screen, which answers unknown callers for you.

Handy things to try:

- "What is on my screen? Summarise it."
- "Find the email from the school about Friday."
- "Translate this sign." Then point your camera at it.

## In your web browser

Browsers are becoming AI-powered. Many now have an AI sidebar that can read the page you are looking at.

Try it: open a long article, open the sidebar, and ask: "Summarise this in five bullets." Or: "What does this page say about prices?" The AI reads the page for you.

Some browsers now have agents that can click for you: fill in forms, compare prices, or book a table. Go slowly with these. AI that clicks buttons is powerful, but it still makes mistakes. Malicious web pages can also try to trick it. Watch what it does. Approve purchases yourself. Never let it loose while logged into your bank.

## Smart speakers and your home

Smart speakers have had AI for years. Alexa, Google Home, and Siri on a HomePod can play music, set timers, answer questions, and control your lights.

The newer versions, like Alexa+, are much better at conversation. They remember context. They can handle follow-ups. "Turn off the kitchen light" now works alongside "What is the weather tomorrow, and should I bring a coat?"

Handy things to try:

- "Set a timer for 20 minutes."
- "Add milk to my shopping list."
- "Turn off all the lights downstairs."
- "What is on my calendar tomorrow?"

## In the car

Cars now have AI assistants built in. They can read your messages aloud, find a petrol station, reroute around traffic, and answer questions about the journey.

Some cars understand natural speech: "I am hungry. Find a good place to eat in the next 20 minutes." The assistant picks somewhere on your route, not just the nearest fast-food place.

A safety note: set up your destination before you drive. Talking to the car is fine. Staring at a screen is not.

## Glasses, watches, and gadgets

Smart glasses can now see what you see and answer questions about it. "What is that building?" "Translate that sign." "Remind me what I am looking at when I get home."

Smart watches use AI to spot patterns in your health: your sleep, your heart rate, your steps. They can warn you about unusual patterns and suggest small changes. They are helpful, not medical. If a watch worries you, see a doctor.

## Turning built-in AI on and off

Built-in AI is handy. It is also on by default, in places you may not expect. It is worth knowing where the switches are.

On an iPhone, look in Settings, then Apple Intelligence and Siri. You can turn features on or off, and choose what Siri may use. On Android, look in Settings, then Gemini or Google Assistant settings. You can pick which apps Gemini may read, like Gmail or Calendar.

In your browser, the AI sidebar usually has a small settings icon. You can turn the sidebar off, or stop it reading the page you are on. In a smart speaker app, you can review what the speaker heard, and delete recordings.

You do not need to turn anything off. Most people leave it all on and enjoy it. But knowing where the switches are means you are choosing, not just accepting. That is the difference between using a tool and being used by one.

One more habit: once a month, look at what your devices have saved. Voice recordings, search history, photos the AI has tagged. Delete what you do not need. It takes five minutes. It keeps your digital house tidy.

> TRY IT: Open the settings on your phone right now. Find the AI settings for your assistant or your voice assistant. Look at what it can access. Turn off one thing you do not need. You have just taken control of your own device.

## In your messaging apps

Meta AI lives inside WhatsApp, Instagram, and Facebook Messenger. You can ask it questions without leaving your chats. It can also make images: type "Imagine..." and describe a picture.

This is handy for quick questions. It is also the place where the safety rules matter most, because chats feel private. They are not. Do not share sensitive details in a messaging-app AI, any more than you would in a chatbot.

> TRY IT: Pick one device you own: your phone, your speaker, or your browser. Find one AI feature on it that you have never used. Try it today. Write down what it did well and what it did badly. You now know more about your own devices than most people do.

## In short

- AI is already in your phone, speaker, car, glasses, browser, and messaging apps.
- iPhone has Apple Intelligence and Siri. Android has Gemini and Galaxy AI.
- Browser sidebars can read and summarise the page you are on.
- Smart speakers, cars, glasses, and watches all use AI every day.
- Try one feature you have never used, on a device you already own.
- The safety rules apply everywhere, even inside friendly apps.
