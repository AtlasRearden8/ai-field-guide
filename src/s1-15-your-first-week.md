# 1.15 Your first week: seven small wins
<!-- original: ch. 15 -->

Reading about AI is nice. Using it to make your real life easier is when it clicks. Here are seven small projects, one a day, each solving a genuine everyday problem while quietly teaching you a core AI skill. Each takes 20 to 30 minutes. Do them in order. By Sunday, you will have planned meals, sent a tricky message, decoded a document, learned something new, planned an outing, made something creative, and built your own helper.

Use any assistant you like. The phone app works best for this week, because you can use voice and photos too.

## Day 1: A week of meals and a shopping list

**Time:** 20 minutes. **Skill:** giving context and constraints.

Tell the AI about your household:

"Plan 5 weeknight dinners for [2 adults and a 10-year-old]. We like [pasta, curries, and tacos], dislike [mushrooms], and I have [30 minutes] to cook on weeknights. Budget-friendly. Ask me any questions first."

Answer its questions. Then steer: "Swap Wednesday for something vegetarian." "Make Friday a fun one."

Then say: "Make a shopping list grouped by supermarket section, and tell me what I can prep on Sunday."

Bonus: "Check my list against what I already have: [rice, pasta, eggs, onions]."

Level up: snap a photo of your fridge and ask: "What can I make tonight with this?"

**What you learned:** context turns generic suggestions into a plan made for you. Who, likes, time, and budget are the four details that matter most.

## Day 2: Tame a tricky message

**Time:** 20 minutes. **Skill:** steering tone with follow-ups.

Think of a message you have been putting off: a complaint, a request, saying no, an apology, or chasing money someone owes.

Explain the situation: "I need to [ask my neighbour to stop parking across my driveway]. We get on okay and I want to keep it friendly, but it has happened 5 times. Draft a short text."

Then steer the tone: "Warmer." "A bit firmer." "Shorter." "Less formal: it is a text, not a letter."

Then ask: "How might they react, and how should I respond to each reaction?"

Edit the final version so it sounds like you. Send it, or save it for the right moment.

**What you learned:** the first draft is a starting point. Steering gets you exactly the right words.

> NOTE: Never send a message you have not read. AI drafts are good. Your judgement is better. Read it aloud before you send it.

## Day 3: Decode a confusing document

**Time:** 20 minutes. **Skill:** uploading files and asking precise questions.

Find something confusing: a bill, an insurance letter, a school policy, an appliance manual, or a lease.

Remove private numbers first: account numbers, ID numbers, passwords. Then upload a photo or a PDF, or paste the text.

Ask: "Explain this in plain English. What is it about, what do I need to do, and by when?"

Then ask precise questions: "Is there anything unusual or worth questioning here?" "Answer only from this document: what is the fee if I cancel early?"

If action is needed, say: "Draft a short reply." Or: "List the steps I should take."

**What you learned:** give AI the real material, ask specific questions, and say "only from this document" for accuracy.

## Day 4: Learn something in 20 minutes

**Time:** 20 minutes. **Skill:** using AI as a tutor.

Pick something you have always wondered about: how the stock market works, why we have time zones, how to read music, or the basics of a language.

Say: "Teach me the basics of [how the stock market works] in 5 short lessons. I am a complete beginner. After each lesson, ask me one question to check I understood, and wait for my answer."

Answer honestly. Say "I don't get it" whenever you do not. It will explain differently.

At the end, say: "Give me a one-page summary and 3 good sources to learn more."

Try part of it in voice mode. It is like a private tutor on the phone.

**What you learned:** AI is an endlessly patient teacher, especially when it quizzes you instead of lecturing.

## Day 5: Plan an outing

**Time:** 30 minutes. **Skill:** planning with web search and fact-checking.

Say: "Plan a [Saturday day trip] from [your town] for [2 adults and a dog]. We like [countryside walks and a good pub lunch]. Up to [1.5 hours] away. Search the web for current info and include links."

Pick a favourite and ask: "Make a timeline for the day with travel times."

Then fact-check. This is the important part. Open the links. Check opening hours, prices, and dog rules on the official websites. Tell the AI anything it got wrong.

Bonus: "What should we pack, and what is the backup plan if it rains?"

**What you learned:** AI plans brilliantly, and you check the facts that matter.

> TRY IT: Do Day 5 even if you do not go on the outing. The plan is the practice. The fact-checking is the skill. Both are worth 30 minutes.

## Day 6: Make something creative

**Time:** 30 minutes. **Skill:** voice and image creation.

Choose one of these. Just one.

**A custom card.** Ask for a funny poem for someone's birthday, plus an AI image of their favourite things. Print it or send it.

**A bedtime story.** Co-write a story with a child. Say: "You start, then my daughter decides what happens next!" Then ask the AI to make a picture of the hero.

**A song or a picture.** Ask for a short song about your pet, or an image of your dream garden. Play with it. Be silly. Creativity is a skill, and play is how you learn it.

**What you learned:** AI is not just for chores. It is a creative partner, and steering it is the same skill as steering a draft.

## Day 7: Build your own helper

**Time:** 30 minutes. **Skill:** custom instructions and reusable assistants.

Today you build something that lasts. A helper is a saved set of instructions that you can reuse.

Pick one job you do often: weekly meal plans, work emails, bedtime stories, or study notes. Write a short instruction for it, like this:

"You are my meal planner. Plan 5 weeknight dinners for 2 adults and a 10-year-old. We like pasta, curries, and tacos. We dislike mushrooms. Cooking time under 30 minutes. Budget-friendly. Always end with a shopping list grouped by supermarket section."

Now save it. Different assistants call this different things: a Project in Claude, a Gem in Gemini, a Space in Perplexity, or a custom assistant elsewhere. Look in the sidebar for "Projects", "Gems", or "Spaces". Paste your instructions there. Or, if your assistant has none of these, save the instruction in a note and paste it at the start of a new chat each time. That works too.

Test it. Start a new chat with your helper and say: "Plan this week." It should just work.

**What you learned:** you can teach the AI your preferences once, and reuse them forever. That is the step from user to owner.

## You did it!

Seven days. Seven wins. Look back at what you made: a meal plan, a tricky message, a decoded document, a new skill, an outing plan, something creative, and a helper of your own.

That is not a small thing. That is a person who uses AI every day, without fear.

Keep the habit going. Chapter 1.16 gives you a 30-day plan that builds on this week. The rest of the book builds on that.

## Check yourself

1. What does Day 1 teach you?
**Answer:** That context, who, likes, time, and budget, turns generic suggestions into a plan made for you.

2. Why should you read a tricky message aloud before you send it?
**Answer:** Because AI drafts are good, but your judgement is better. Reading aloud catches tone problems.

3. What three words make file questions more accurate?
**Answer:** "Only from this document." They tell the AI to stick to the file, not to guess.

4. Why is Day 5's fact-checking step so important?
**Answer:** Because opening hours, prices, and rules change, and AI can get them wrong. You check the facts that matter.

5. What is a helper, and how do you build one?
**Answer:** A saved set of instructions for a job you do often. Write the instructions once, save them as a Project, Gem, Space, or note, and reuse them.

## In short

- One small project a day, for seven days, teaches you more than a week of reading.
- Each day teaches one core skill: context, tone, files, tutoring, fact-checking, creativity, and helpers.
- Always read before you send, and check the facts that matter.
- Day 7's helper is the big step: teach it once, reuse it forever.
- Seven wins in a week means you are now a daily AI user.
