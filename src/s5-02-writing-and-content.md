# 5.2 AI for writing and content
<!-- original: ch. 92 -->

AI can help you write. It can also make you sound like everyone else. The difference is how you use it. Use AI for the chores. Keep your voice for the parts that matter. That is the whole chapter in one line.

Writing has chores and craft. Chores are the boring parts. Outlining. Fixing grammar. Shortening a draft. Writing ten versions of a headline. Craft is the thinking part. Your ideas. Your stories. Your judgement about what matters. AI is good at chores. You are good at craft. Split the work that way and you get the best of both.

## The creator's workflow

A good writing workflow has five stages. AI helps most in stages one and four. You lead stages two, three, and five.

Stage one is ideas. Ask AI for twenty angles on a topic. Ask for ten headlines. Ask what questions readers ask most. Pick the best two. Throw the rest away. AI is a brainstorming partner, not an editor-in-chief.

Stage two is the messy draft. Write it yourself, fast and ugly. Do not polish. Do not stop. Get the ideas down. A draft written by you has your thoughts in it. A draft written by AI has the internet's average thoughts in it. There is a difference, and readers can feel it.

Stage three is structure. Ask AI to read your messy draft and suggest an outline. Does the argument flow? What is missing? What should move? Use its suggestions. Keep your decisions.

Stage four is the edit. This is AI's highest-value job. Ask it to fix grammar. Ask it to cut repetition. Ask it to shorten by a third. Ask it to flag sentences a reader would stumble on. Take the fixes. Reject the ones that change your meaning.

Stage five is the final read. Read the piece out loud. Your ear catches what your eye misses. If a sentence sounds like a robot wrote it, rewrite it yourself. Then publish.

> NOTE: AI text has a tell. It loves phrases like "in today's fast-paced world" and "it is important to note that". If your draft is full of those, it is not your voice. Cut them.

## Teaching AI your voice

AI writes in a generic voice by default. You can teach it yours. It takes a little setup and it pays off for months.

First, collect three pieces you are proud of. Paste them into a chat. Ask the AI to describe your voice. What is the sentence length? The tone? The words you use and avoid? Save that description. It becomes your style guide.

Second, use the style guide in every writing prompt. "Write in this style" plus the guide beats "write well" every time. Give examples, not adjectives. "Write like this paragraph" works. "Write engagingly" does not.

Third, banish AI voice directly. Tell the AI what to avoid. No clichés. No "delve". No "moreover". No sentences that start with "In conclusion". Short sentences. Plain words. Your list of banned phrases will grow. Keep it in your style guide.

For creators, special formats matter. A newsletter needs a hook in the first two lines. A video script needs short spoken sentences. A social post needs one idea and one ending. Tell the AI the format and its rules before you ask it to draft. A prompt without a format gets a generic essay.

## The editor, and the repurposing machine

The AI editor is the highest-value use in this chapter. Here is how to use it well.

Ask for a specific edit, not a general one. "Cut this by 30 percent and keep every fact" is a good prompt. "Make this better" is a bad one. Ask it to explain each change it makes. If it cannot explain a change, do not accept it. Ask it to check facts against your sources. Ask it to find repetition. Ask it to read for a specific reader. "Would a busy parent understand this?" is a sharper question than "Is this clear?"

Repurposing turns one piece into many. One long article can become five short posts, a newsletter, a video outline, and a set of questions for a talk. Ask AI to do the splitting. Then rewrite each piece in your voice. The idea stays yours. The formats multiply.

Learning from your audience is the last piece. Paste in comments or feedback. Ask what readers loved and what confused them. Ask what to write next. Your audience is a better editor than any model. AI just helps you hear them faster.

> TRY IT: Take something you wrote in the past. Ask AI to describe your voice from it, then ask it to rewrite a new paragraph in that voice. Read both versions out loud. Keep the sentences that sound like you. Delete the ones that do not.

## Ethics and trust

Writing with AI has rules. They are simple, and they matter.

Be honest with readers. If AI helped you, say so where it matters. A short note is enough. "Drafted with AI help, edited by me." Readers respect honesty. They resent being fooled.

Do not fake experience. AI can write a review of a product you never used. That is lying. Do not publish it. Do not fake quotes. Do not fake interviews. Do not fake expertise. Your name is on the piece. AI is not accountable. You are.

Check facts before you publish. AI invents statistics, quotes, and studies. A wrong number in your article is your wrong number. Verify anything a reader might act on. Check names, dates, and figures in the original source.

Respect other writers. Do not ask AI to copy a living writer's style and pass it off as yours. Do not paste someone else's article into AI and republish it as your own. That is theft with extra steps.

Special formats each have their own rules. A newsletter needs a hook in the first two lines, or readers close it. A video script needs short spoken sentences, because ears are not eyes. A social post needs one idea and one clear ending. A product page needs the benefit before the feature. Tell the AI the format and its rules before you ask for a draft. A prompt without a format gets a generic essay. A prompt with a format gets a usable draft.

Learning from your audience is the last piece. Paste in comments, replies, or feedback. Ask three questions. What did readers love? What confused them? What should I write next? The answers show you what to repeat and what to fix. Your audience is a better editor than any model. AI just helps you hear them faster. Thank the people who give honest feedback. They are doing free work for you.

Headlines deserve their own step. A good headline promises one clear thing. Ask AI for ten headlines for your piece. Pick the clearest two. Test them on a friend. "Would you click this?" If the answer is no, try again. A clear headline beats a clever one. Clever confuses. Clear invites.

## Check yourself

1. Which parts of writing should AI do, and which should you keep?
**Answer:** AI should do the chores like outlining, grammar, and shortening, while you keep the craft: your ideas, stories, and judgement.

2. Why write the messy draft yourself?
**Answer:** Because a draft written by you holds your thoughts, while an AI draft holds the internet's average thoughts.

3. How do you teach AI your voice?
**Answer:** Give it three pieces you are proud of, ask it to describe your voice, save that as a style guide, and use it in every prompt.

4. What is the highest-value use of AI for writers?
**Answer:** Editing: fixing grammar, cutting repetition, shortening, and flagging sentences a reader would stumble on.

5. What is the honesty rule for publishing AI-assisted writing?
**Answer:** Say AI helped where it matters, never fake experience or quotes, and check every fact before you publish.

## In short

- Use AI for writing chores, and keep the craft, your ideas and voice, for yourself.
- Write the messy draft yourself, then let AI help with structure and editing.
- Build a style guide from your best work, and ban AI clichés from it.
- Repurpose one piece into many formats, rewriting each in your voice.
- Be honest that AI helped, and never publish faked experience or unchecked facts.
- Try this week: a style guide, one AI-edited draft, one repurposed piece, and a read-aloud test.
